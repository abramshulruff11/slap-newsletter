CHECK 1 — BANNED PHRASES AND CONSTRUCTIONS (fix automatically)
================================================================================

--- SECTION A: THE DUELING SENTENCE CONSTRUCTION ---
This is the #1 priority fix. Scan the entire draft for this pattern before
checking anything else.

THE PATTERN: A sentence saying what something ISN'T, followed by a sentence
redefining it as something more dramatic.

Detect any sentence matching these forms:
- "This wasn't [X]. This was [Y]."
- "That's not [X]. That's [Y]."
- "This isn't [X]. This is [Y]."
- "That wasn't [X]. That was [Y]."
- "This wasn't just [X]. This was [Y]."
- "That's not just [X]. That's [Y]."
- Any two-sentence pair where sentence 1 negates a noun and sentence 2
  redefines it with a more dramatic noun.

Examples to catch:
- "This wasn't a basketball game. This was a public execution."
- "That's not a loss. That's a disaster."
- "This isn't a slump. This is a collapse."
- "That wasn't a trade. That was a robbery."

When you find this construction: DELETE BOTH SENTENCES. Replace them with
the most specific factual detail from the surrounding context that makes
the point without editorializing. The fact is always more powerful.

BAD: "This wasn't a basketball game. This was a public execution."
GOOD: "The Hawks scored 36 points at halftime. OG Anunoby had 26."

Do not soften this fix. Do not keep one sentence. Delete both and replace
with a fact. This construction has no valid form in SLAP.

CHECK 5 — TWEET-TO-PROSE DUPLICATION (fix automatically)
================================================================================

For every tweet blockquote embedded in a section, scan the prose immediately
before and after it in the same section.

The rule: prose must not restate, paraphrase, or summarize what the tweet
already shows. Commentary is fine. Repetition is not.

- Prose BEFORE a tweet = setup or context. It should tell the reader why the
  tweet matters, not describe what the tweet says.
- Prose AFTER a tweet = reaction or pivot. It should add a new angle, not
  recap the tweet's content.

BAD: Tweet shows a player's stat line. Prose after: "As you can see, [Player]
put up an incredible [stat] performance in the game."

GOOD: Tweet shows a player's stat line. Prose after: "The last time someone
did that in a playoff game, Jordan was in shorts."

When you find duplication — prose that describes the tweet's own content —
delete the duplicating sentence(s). Do not replace them.

CHECK 7 — PUNCHING DOWN (auto-remove)
================================================================================

Scan every <p> tag for any sentence whose joke or comparison targets:
- A disability or medical condition (stuttering, dyslexia, autism, ADHD,
  physical disability)
- Race, ethnicity, or national origin
- Religion
- Sexual orientation or gender identity
- Body type or weight
- A mental health diagnosis (depression, anxiety, OCD, PTSD)
- An athlete's family member who isn't themselves a public figure
- Death or terminal illness used as a punchline

CARVE-OUTS — the following are NOT punching down. Do NOT remove these:
- Body type or size discussed as direct athletic context with no mockery:
  "the offensive line gave up 200 pounds at every position," "Wemby's 7'4"
  frame," "6'5" Mahomes can see over the line"
- A team's geographic location used as part of a team-level joke: "New York
  fans," "Bay Area front offices," "Boston sports radio"
- A player's nationality stated factually as part of their identity as an
  athlete: "the 20-year-old Irish rugby convert," "the Slovenian point guard"
- Self-targeted humor by the writer
- Jokes about a public figure's professional decisions or on-field actions
- Jokes about a team's performance, owners, executives, or coaching

THE TEST: is the joke's punchline the target's IDENTITY (a thing they ARE)
or their ACTIONS (a thing they DID)? Identity → cut. Actions → leave.

When you find a sentence whose punchline targets identity:
DELETE the sentence and replace with this comment:

<!-- EDITOR FLAG: PUNCHING DOWN — removed: "[quote of cut sentence]" — joke targeted [category]. Writer needs a different angle. -->

This is HARD REMOVAL, not a soft flag. The line cannot ship. The flag tells
the writer what was cut so they can rewrite the section if needed.

CHECK 8 — UNSOURCED FACTUAL CLAIMS (fix or cut; NEVER flag)
================================================================================

A wrong fact published is worse than a duller sentence. This check used to
insert a VERIFY comment for a human to read and left the claim standing;
nobody read the comments, so it protected nothing while producing 15+ of them
an issue. The check is not gone — the annotating is. It now ACTS on what it
finds, and adds no comment at all.

WHAT COUNTS AS SOURCED — leave these completely alone:

  1. An embedded tweet in the SAME section states it. A StatMuse line or a beat
     reporter's number carries its author's byline; that is a source.
  2. The GROUND TRUTH block at the top of this message states it. That block is
     yesterday's real results from the ESPN API. If it is absent, or silent on
     this particular claim, it does not source the claim.
  3. It carries no specific number, year, title or league placement. A general
     statement is not checkable and is not this check's business.

Anything else in the four categories below is UNSOURCED. Change the sentence so
the unverifiable part is gone and everything still standing is kept. Never
delete a whole paragraph. Never alter text inside a <blockquote>. Never leave a
comment explaining what you changed.

--- CATEGORY A: CONFERENCE / DIVISION PLACEMENT ---
    "the AFC East-leading Bills"  ->  "the Bills"
    "an NFC team for the first time"  ->  cut the clause
Cut the placement. Keep the rest of the sentence exactly as written.

--- CATEGORY B: TITLE AND CHAMPIONSHIP CLAIMS ---
Trigger words: defending, reigning, current champion(s), back-to-back,
three-peat, "X-time champion", or any claim that someone currently holds a title.
    "the defending champion Thunder"  ->  "the Thunder"
    "back-to-back MVP Jokic"  ->  "Jokic"
Cut the title phrase. Keep the subject.

--- CATEGORY C: HISTORICAL FIRSTS, STREAKS AND DROUGHTS ---
Any "first since YYYY", "most since", "won N straight", "longest in N years".
Replace the specific figure with relative framing, which cannot be factually
wrong:
    "their first Finals since 1987"  ->  "their first Finals in decades"
    "won 8 straight"                 ->  "on a long winning streak"
If relative framing leaves the sentence with no point, cut the clause instead.
This is RULE 3 of rolling_feedback.txt — a specific wrong number is far worse
than a vague right impression — enforced here instead of hoped for.

--- CATEGORY D: SPECIFIC PERFORMANCE NUMBERS IN PROSE ---
Scoring lines, season totals, game counts, franchise records.
    If a tweet in the same section carries the number: LEAVE IT.
    If the ground truth block carries it: LEAVE IT.
    Otherwise cut the number and keep the sentence standing:
        "Judge is hitting .318 with 44 homers"
        ->  "Judge is having the best year in that lineup"
    When the number IS the entire sentence, cut the sentence.

--- THE ONE THING TO GET RIGHT ---
Do not strip a number just because you cannot personally confirm it. Strip it
when it is the kind of claim that CAN be checked and nothing in this document
checks it. A stat lifted from the tweet directly beneath it is sourced, and
cutting it makes the issue worse, not safer.
