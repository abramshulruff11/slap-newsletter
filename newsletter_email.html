76ers Spoil Ring Night at the Garden

The Knicks raised their first championship banner in five decades. Mike Breen was on the mic. Walt Frazier handed out the rings. Ewing and Starks were in the stands. Reggie Miller — Reggie Miller — sat there and watched New York put a title banner in the rafters. Then Philadelphia walked in and won by six.

LeBron's debut as a Sixer: 14-9-7. Most points, rebounds and assists by a 40+ year old in franchise history. At 41 he's not trying to be the guy. He's the point guard who makes the guys the guy.

Maxey led with 28. Paul George dropped 38 — the game-high. Jaylen Brown chipped in 19-8-6 in his first game in a Sixers uniform. Embiid did Embiid things. Four All-Stars, four scoring threats, and the one who ran the show is the one who was already there.

Jalen Brunson had 15 at the half. Second half: 6 points on 2-for-10 shooting.

The ceremony itself was perfect. Walt Frazier presented the rings before the first banner in five decades rose at Madison Square Garden. Josh Hart had his hair braided to look like the Larry O'Brien trophy. Tracy Morgan was drunk in the arena doing Tracy Morgan things. The Knicks also quietly hung an NBA Cup banner a few hours before the game after initially deciding not to — unclear what changed, but when you're hanging one you might as well hang two.

Jaylen Brown threw a lob to LeBron and immediately tried to recreate the LeBron-Wade photo in his very first game as a teammate.

Thunder Make the WCF Rematch Look Easy

Oklahoma City went into San Antonio and won by 12. The score was 116-104 but it wasn't that close — the Thunder led by 21 at halftime and the Spurs' late push just made the final look respectable.

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander: 32-5-4, two steals, two blocks, 13-of-27 from the floor. He passed Charles Barkley on the all-time 30-point games list in the first game of the season.

Victor Wembanyama: 1-for-13 from the floor. Zero makes until a late-first-half dunk. The Thunder threw Chet Holmgren, Isaiah Hartenstein, and anyone else over 6'10" at him in waves.

McDavid on Pace for 242

Nine games into the season Connor McDavid has 26 points. Six goals, twenty assists. The 20 assists alone would lead the league in total points if assists counted as full points.

Extrapolate the pace and you get 242 points. Wayne Gretzky's single-season record is 215. Nobody has come within 50 points of that number in nearly four decades. McDavid is nine games into the season and running at a pace that would obliterate it.

October paces don't survive the full season. But Edmonton is 7-2, their best start in years, and he's doing it against actual NHL opposition in actual NHL games.

Ohio State Loses Its QB

Julian Sayin is out indefinitely. Ryan Day indicated the Buckeyes could be without their starting quarterback for the rest of the season. Luke Fahey is next up. Jeremiah Smith is the best receiver in college football and now has to be the entire offense.
