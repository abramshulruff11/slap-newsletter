The Boo Becomes a Roar

LeBron James made his home debut as a Philadelphia 76er on Thursday night. By halftime, his new fans were booing him off the floor.

Philly was down 69-45 at the break. Jaylen Brown had four points. The Sixers turned it over 15 times in the first half — their most in a half since January 2024. The deficit got as big as 29. The crowd that was supposed to coronate a Big 4 was busy practicing its goodbyes.

Philly fans at halftime, watching the LeBron era begin with a 24-point hole:

Then the second half happened. The 76ers took their first lead of the game, 96-95, on an Embiid basket with 6:54 remaining in the fourth. The starters scored 64 of 69 second-half points. Maxey dropped 17 in the second half after holding things together for 15 in the first. The building that was booing turned into a riot.

https://twitter.com/NBAMemes/status/2113449848737505770

NBA teams had won 466 consecutive regular-season games after leading by 29 or more points. Cleveland had been 155-0 in such situations. The Cavs' choke job was the first time in the shot clock era that a team had led by 24 points at the half of their season opener and lost.

The 29-point comeback was the Sixers' largest in franchise history. The previous high was 26 against Indiana in 2008.

Maxey and Donovan Mitchell both finished with 32. Identical stat lines, completely different stories. Mitchell had 22 in the first half torching Philly into submission, then went quiet with 10 in the second as his team hemorrhaged the lead. Maxey had 15 first, 17 second — the steady hand all night, and the guy screaming at the crowd when the 3 fell to cut it to 10.

Cleveland fans this morning, consulting the ledger that just read 155-0:

LeBron's night: 17 points, 10 rebounds, five assists and three steals. He wasn't the leading scorer. He wasn't the hero. But it was the largest comeback of James' career. The city booed him at the half and chaired him by the end.

The Streak SGA Chose to End

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander had 141 straight regular-season games with 20+ points. On Thursday night in a blowout of the Nuggets, he asked out at 17 and ended it himself.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2113486862866411828

The MVP sitting in a 96-84 win rather than milking three more buckets in garbage time to keep a historic streak alive. In a league that gave us Westbrook stat-padding to the final buzzer for triple-doubles, it's genuinely weird to see a superstar look at a record on the table and say nah.

The other half of this story is that OKC absolutely smothered Denver. Jokic went 5-19 from the floor for 13 points. The two-time MVP got walled off by Chet, Hartenstein and a Thunder defense that just doesn't let anything comfortable happen in the paint.

https://twitter.com/NBAMemes/status/2113518067519312254

Jalen Williams quietly led OKC with 21. The Thunder are 2-0. Their best player voluntarily ended a 141-game streak and they still won by 12.

The Kick That Almost Wasn't

Cairo Santos missed a fourth-quarter field goal that would've put the Bears up two scores on the Patriots. Ben Johnson sent him back out later with the game on the line, and he drilled a 58-yarder to seal it.

https://twitter.com/SleeperHQ/status/2113469405183324305

Caleb Williams went 22-of-33 for 308 and 2 TDs with zero picks. Luther Burden III caught 4 for 130 and a score — and he's now caught every single one of his 17 targets this season. Perfect catch rate through seven weeks.

The fourth quarter was Ben Johnson football distilled: 12:21 of possession, 24 plays to New England's 8, 110 yards to 36. Chicago is 5-2, their best start since 2020, and sits one game back in the NFC North. The Bears are a real team again. Nobody tell them.

Faulk's Fight

Pro Football Hall of Fame running back and Southern University head coach Marshall Faulk revealed Wednesday that he has been diagnosed with Parkinson's disease.

Faulk, 53, revealed his diagnosis on a podcast Wednesday. He said that with medication, he is not experiencing "issues" beyond hand rigidity, which he believes hasn't gotten worse in the year and a half he has been dealing with the disease, and "little tremors." He discussed the diagnosis during an episode of The Caring Neurologist podcast with Dr. Sandeep Thakkar, released Wednesday, Oct. 21.

Faulk told Dr. Sandeep Thakkar on the show "The Caring Neurologist" that in discussing his illness, "I want to help as many people as I can." Faulk said he received his diagnosis in the early stages of the disease and considers himself "one of the lucky ones" for doing so.

Faulk played more than 10 seasons in the NFL, first with the Indianapolis Colts and then with the St. Louis Rams, with whom he won a Super Bowl and was named Most Valuable Player. After major knee surgery, he retired in 2007. Four years later, in his first year of eligibility, he was elected to the Pro Football Hall of Fame. He is currently in his first season as head coach at Southern after serving as Deion Sanders' running backs coach at Colorado.
