NBA Passes Radical Anti-Tanking Reform: 3-2-1 Lottery, Relegation Zone, Flattened Odds

@ShamsCharania
Breaking: The NBA's Board of Governors has passed new anti-tanking rules that include expanding the draft lottery from 14 to 16 teams, a relegation zone where the bottom 3 teams get penalized with lessened chances for the No. 1 pick, and flattened odds, sources tell ESPN.
View tweet

The NBA Board of Governors passed sweeping lottery reform Thursday by a 29-1 vote. The "3-2-1 lottery" expands the draft lottery from 14 to 16 teams, flattens odds, and creates a relegation zone that penalizes the bottom three teams with reduced chances at the No. 1 pick.

@ShamsCharania
The NBA's Board of Governors vote passed 29-1 -- with the Memphis Grizzlies as the lone vote against the new anti-tanking lottery reform, sources tell ESPN.
View tweet

Memphis voted no because they own a swap with Utah for the 2027 Draft. Utah got top-5 picks in back-to-back years. Under the new rules — which prevent teams from winning three consecutive top-5 picks — the pick Memphis would receive is now capped at 6th. Memphis voted against their own future asset.

@ShamsCharania
The NBA makes an unprecedented move in American pro sports league by punishing the very worst teams in the draft lottery and creating a new system to incentivize winning — after months of meetings and conversations among league, ownership and team stakeholders. A radical response from the NBA to radical behavior, with nearly 8-to-10 teams jockeying for lottery odds down stretch of this season. The new "3-2-1" lottery starts next Draft and lasts through at least 2029.
View tweet

The bottom three teams get two lottery balls each. Teams 4-10 get three balls. The 9th and 10th play-in seeds get two balls. Losers of the 7-8 play-in games get one ball. No team can win the No. 1 pick in consecutive years or win three consecutive top-5 picks.

First American pro sports league to actively punish the bottom teams in the draft. Starts with the 2027 Draft, runs through at least 2029. Tanking is dead.

Claude Lemieux, Four-Time Stanley Cup Champion and 1995 Conn Smythe Winner, Dies by Suicide at 60

Claude Lemieux, who won four Stanley Cups across a 21-year NHL career and captured the 1995 Conn Smythe Trophy as playoff MVP, died by suicide at age 60. The NHL Alumni Association and his family confirmed Thursday.

Lemieux was known for his tenacity, his ability to get under opponents' skin, and his clutch playoff performances. He won championships with Montreal (1986), New Jersey (1995, 2000), and Colorado (1996). The 1995 Conn Smythe came after he led the Devils with 13 goals in 20 games during their first championship run. He finished his career with 786 games, 379 points, and a reputation as one of the game's fiercest competitors.

If you or someone you know is struggling with thoughts of suicide, the National Suicide Prevention Lifeline is available 24/7 at 988.
