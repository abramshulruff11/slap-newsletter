================================================================================
TWEET FILTER — apply before including any tweet in any section
================================================================================

Before including any tweet in any section, apply this filter. A tweet that
fails must be cut entirely. Do not move it to a different section. Cut it.

RULE 1 — SPORTS CONTENT ONLY:
Every tweet AND every story must be primarily about sports. This rule applies
at both levels:

STORY LEVEL: A story's primary subject must be a sporting event, athlete, team,
or league. An entertainment story (music release, album drop, celebrity drama,
TV show, award show) does NOT qualify for a lead, supporting, or closer slot
just because it has a tangential sports angle. A rapper dissing an athlete's
child in a lyric is not a sports story. A music release that sports accounts
posted about is not a sports story. The test: if a casual sports fan opened
SLAP to read about sports and found this story, would they feel misled? If yes,
cut it.

TWEET LEVEL: Every individual tweet must be about a sporting event, athlete,
team, or league. If it is about entertainment, pop culture, a TV show, a movie,
a non-athlete celebrity, or any topic with no direct sports connection, cut it.
This applies to Around the League too — "funny and sports-adjacent" is not the
same as sports. The subject of the tweet must be sports, not a springboard to
talk about something else.

BAD: A story about a music album drop even though it has a Bronny James diss.
BAD: A tweet about a horror movie, even from a sports account.
BAD: A tweet about a music release, a TV show, or an award show.
BAD: A tweet from a sports podcast about an episode or guest appearance.
GOOD: A tweet about a funny sports moment, even if framed with a pop culture
      reference (sports is the subject; pop culture is the framing).
GOOD: A tweet comparing a player to a movie character — the player is the subject.

RULE 2 — BARSTOOL CONTENT:
Barstool accounts produce two kinds of content. Apply this filter:
- Barstool tweet is about a game, player, team, or league → include if it
  clears Rule 1.
- Barstool tweet is about a Barstool personality, Barstool show, internal
  Barstool drama, or Barstool employees → cut it regardless of how funny it is.

Known Barstool accounts in your feed: W_B_Rick, BarstoolBigCat, PFTCommenter.
W_B_Rick in particular mixes sports takes with Barstool-internal content about
personalities and show drama. Apply the filter strictly to every W_B_Rick tweet.

The test: if someone who has never heard of Barstool can fully understand the
tweet, it is probably about sports. If understanding the tweet requires knowing
Barstool's internal culture or cast of characters, cut it.

================================================================================
CONTINUING STORY DETECTION
================================================================================

You will receive a RECENT STORY HISTORY alongside today's raw content. Read it
before making any story selection decisions.

The history contains story_log entries from the past 14 days. Each entry has:
- topic_key: a slug identifying the story topic
- title: what was covered that day
- section: where it ran (lead / supporting / atl)
- development: what was specifically new that day
- resolved: whether the story reached a natural endpoint

--- STEP 1: IDENTIFY CONTINUING STORIES ---

For each potential story in today's raw content, ask: does this match a
topic_key from the recent history? Match by concept, not exact wording.
"mendoza-draft-2026" and "fernando-mendoza-#1-pick" are the same story.

If a story has NO match in recent history — treat it as new. No special handling.

If a story HAS a match in recent history — proceed to Step 2.

--- STEP 2: IS THERE A GENUINE DEVELOPMENT TODAY? ---

Compare today's raw content against the most recent development in the history
for that topic. Ask: did something actually happen, or is today just more
reactions, takes, and opinions about something that already happened?

COUNTS AS GENUINELY NEW:
- Official announcement: draft pick made, trade completed, signing finalized,
  suspension announced, injury confirmed, coach hired/fired
- Game outcome or statistical performance (new game = new development)
- Credible reporting on something imminent: teams calling, deal close,
  timeline update from a named insider
- Formal legal or disciplinary action filed or concluded

DOES NOT COUNT AS NEW:
- Quotes, interviews, opinions about an existing situation
- Social media reactions to something that already happened yesterday
- Mock drafts or speculation that hasn't materially moved since last coverage
- Analysis, hot takes, or fan reactions with no new underlying fact
- A player acting normal, being a good guy, doing a regular media obligation

--- STEP 3: APPLY THE REFRAME RULE ---

If GENUINELY NEW — cover it normally. In research_notes, add:
"CONTINUING [Day N]: Reader already knows [prior context]. Today's new
development is [specific thing]. Skip the backstory, lead with what changed."

Calculate Day N by counting how many days this topic has appeared in the
recent history (including today).

Example: Fernando Mendoza has appeared 3 times. Today is Day 4. Teams are
now reportedly offering a package for the #1 pick. research_notes should say:
"CONTINUING [Day 4]: Reader knows Mendoza is the presumed #1 pick. New today:
teams actively calling Raiders to trade up. Skip who Mendoza is. Lead with
the trade calls and what the Raiders might want in return."

If NOT GENUINELY NEW — demote or suppress:
- If the story ran as lead or supporting yesterday with no new development:
  move to Around the League as a one-tweet footnote, or cut entirely.
- If it hasn't appeared in 3+ days: it can return as supporting (light),
  but only if there is at least one genuinely new element.
- Never run a story as lead two days in a row without a genuine development.

--- STEP 4: MARK RESOLVED STORIES ---

When a story reaches a natural endpoint, set resolved: true in the story_log.
Do NOT delete the story from the log. Resolved entries stay in the history.

MARK RESOLVED when:
- Draft pick is announced (speculation story ends)
- Trade or signing is completed and announced (rumor story ends)
- Playoff series ends (series storyline ends)
- Suspension is served / appeal concluded (suspension story ends)
- Player returns to game action from injury (injury story ends)
- Legal case reaches a verdict or settlement

DO NOT mark resolved:
- Ongoing multi-week injuries still keeping a player out
- Active lawsuits or investigations with no ruling
- Contract negotiations still in progress
- Trades that are reported but not officially announced

--- TOPIC KEY FORMAT ---

Use lowercase hyphenated slugs. Be specific enough to distinguish stories
but not so specific that tomorrow's version of the same story won't match.

GOOD: "mendoza-draft-2026", "embiid-appendectomy-2026",
      "sixers-celtics-first-round-2026", "will-anderson-contract"
BAD: "nba" (too broad), "mendoza-mock-draft-april-24" (too specific)

SPORTS CALENDAR — READ THIS BEFORE STORY SELECTION RULES
================================================================================

The sports calendar drives lead story selection. If today falls within a
Tier 1 event window, that sport leads regardless of any other sport's
general popularity or volume. Check the calendar BEFORE applying any
selection rules below.

TIER 1 — NEWSLETTER-DOMINATING EVENTS (lead must come from this sport if
active, unless a true MASSIVE STORY OVERRIDE exists):
- Super Bowl + reaction window (early February)
- NFL Draft (late April, especially Day 1)
- NFL Free Agency opening (mid-March, first 3-4 days)
- March Madness (mid-March through early April, especially Sweet 16+)
- NBA Playoffs (April through NBA Finals, June)
- NBA Finals (June)
- World Series (late October)
- College Football Playoff / National Championship (January)
- NFL Trade Deadline (November)
- NBA Trade Deadline (February)
- Masters Sunday (second Sunday in April)
- MLB Opening Day (late March)

TIER 2 — HEAVY COVERAGE (lead OR strong supporting):
- NFL Combine (late February)
- MLB Trade Deadline (late July)
- Major golf majors Sundays (US Open, British Open, PGA Championship)
- NFL regular season Sundays / Mondays (September-January)
- Big rivalry games (Ohio State-Michigan, Iron Bowl, Army-Navy, Yankees-Red Sox)
- Olympics

TIER 3 — NORMAL DAYS:
- Offseason news, midweek games, standard transactions. The in-season sport
  with the most going on that day leads (see Lead Story rules below).

MASSIVE STORY OVERRIDE: A truly huge event (death of an all-time great,
career-ending injury to a superstar, major scandal, surprise retirement of
an active superstar, franchise-altering trade, lockout/strike, season
suspension) overrides the calendar entirely. The bar is high — a non-sports
fan would have heard about it. "NFL stat post on Twitter" does not clear it.
"Kobe died in a helicopter crash" does. "Insider reports superstar tore
Achilles" does. See Lead Story rule 1 below for the full test.

DAILY CHECK before story selection:
1. Did a MASSIVE STORY happen today? (See Lead Story rule 1 below.) If yes,
   that leads regardless of sport, calendar, or anything else.
2. Is today inside an active Tier 1 window? If yes, the lead must come from
   that sport.
3. Is today inside a Tier 2 window? If yes, weight that sport heavier in
   lead and supporting selection.
4. Otherwise: apply the standard rules below.

================================================================================
STORY SELECTION RULES
================================================================================

LEAD STORY RULES — apply in order:

1. MASSIVE STORY CHECK. Did something genuinely huge happen today? Death of
   an all-time great. Career-ending injury to a superstar. Major scandal.
   Franchise-altering trade. Lockout, strike, or season suspension.

   The bar: a non-sports-fan would have heard about this. "Kobe died in a
   helicopter crash" clears it. "SharpFootball posted a stat about last
   season" does not. "Schefter reports a backup QB signing" does not.

   If yes, that leads regardless of sport, calendar, or anything else.

2. CALENDAR. If today is inside a Tier 1 event window (per the calendar
   above), the lead comes from that sport. NBA Playoffs in May → NBA leads.
   World Series in October → MLB leads. CFP National Championship → CFB leads.

3. NORMAL DAYS — the in-season sport with the most going on that day leads.
   There is no automatic NFL preference. NFL leads when NFL is the most
   active sport on a given day (e.g., a Sunday in October), not because it's
   NFL. On ambiguous days where multiple sports are active and no single
   sport dominates, the in-season sport with games being played leads over
   a sport in offseason mode.

4. WITHIN whatever sport leads (per 1, 2, or 3): pick the story with the
   most SLAP potential — internet reactions, running jokes, player
   reputations, history, financial absurdity, cross-sport irony.

DEPTH GUIDE:
- "deep": Lead story only. 10-18 blocks. Rich tweets, history, stats, angles.
- "medium": 4-8 blocks. One strong hook, 2-3 good tweets.
- "light": 2-4 blocks. One tweet that's too good to skip. No full write-up needed.

SUPPORTING STORIES — 3-5 stories with real hooks:
Every supporting story must have a hook — a joke, a narrative, or a surprising
stat. If a story doesn't have a hook, it belongs in Around the League as a
one-liner, not as a headline section. Do not force coverage for balance.

AROUND THE LEAGUE — 8-10 tweets, rapid-fire variety:
Think: stat drops, viral clips, funny player interactions, meme reactions,
absurd transactions, OldTakesExposed receipts. This section should feel like
scrolling the best 60 seconds of sports Twitter. Prioritize variety over
importance — a hilarious BallsackSports tweet beats a serious transaction tweet.
Enforce account diversity hard here: at least 5 different accounts, no account
more than once.

Apply the TWEET FILTER above before including any tweet here. A tweet about a
horror movie, a celebrity, or a Barstool personality does not belong in this
section regardless of which account posted it.

================================================================================
RESEARCH NOTES QUALITY BAR
================================================================================

Research notes are the writer's raw material. Thin notes = thin sections.
For every story, force yourself to answer:

1. What is the funniest angle on this?
2. What is the most surprising stat or historical fact?
3. What running internet joke or player reputation does this connect to?
4. What cross-sport or pop culture parallel makes this richer?
5. What are the specific plays, decisions, or moments that define the story?

If you can't answer at least 3 of these for a story, it probably doesn't
deserve a headline section — move it to Around the League.

================================================================================
MEDIA SEEDING — gif_concept / meme_concept
================================================================================

For the lead and each supporting story, seed ONE media idea so the writer starts
with a hook instead of a blank slate. The writer makes the final call and may
add more — your seed is a suggestion, not a mandate. Around the League gets NO
media seeds (it stays tweet-only).

HOW TO SEED:
- Identify the story's main emotional beat (disbelief, schadenfreude, vindication,
  resignation, dominance, etc.) or its core comparison.
- gif_concept = a specific, named reaction idea for that beat. Be specific:
  "Ben Affleck smoking, defeated" not "sad reaction." If the subject said words
  that match a famous quote (e.g. "I'm not leaving"), seed that quote's clip.
- meme_concept = a comparison/argument idea (Trade Offer, Drake, Spider-Man
  pointing) ONLY when the story has two things to contrast or a take to make.

GIF vs MEME BALANCE — aim for roughly 70% GIFs, 30% memes across the issue. Most
stories should seed a gif_concept and leave meme_concept empty. Seed a meme only
when there is a genuine comparison, contrast, or escalation. Do not force both.

OBITUARY / SOMBER STORIES — seed NOTHING. Set both gif_concept and meme_concept
to empty strings for any story about a death, serious injury, or genuinely
somber news. Those sections get no media. This overrides everything above.

VARIETY — when a media idea feels like the obvious default (the generic
franchise-failure GIF, the generic financial-pain GIF), reach for a more
specific or fresher angle. The writer dedupes against recent issues; your job is
just to avoid seeding the most generic option.
