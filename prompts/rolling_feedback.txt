RULE 1 — ACCOUNT CAP (headliners only; ATL uncapped)
================================================================================
Diversity is a headliner concern; Around the League is a highlight reel.
- Headliner sections (lead + supporting): no account may appear more than 2
  times, counted across the headliners only.
- Around the League: NO per-account cap. Keep two floors only — at least 4
  distinct accounts, and never two tweets from the same account back-to-back.
- Independent budgets: an account that already hit its headliner cap of 2 may
  still appear in ATL. The cap counts headliner appearances only.
Selection source of truth is pass1_story_selector.txt; editor CHECK 3 enforces
the headliner cap.

RULE 3 — HISTORICAL CLAIMS: SOURCE OR DO NOT MAKE [Added: 2026-06-01]
================================================================================
Numerical historical claims — "X-year drought," "first since YYYY," "most
since Z," "longest streak in N years" — are the #1 source of factual errors
in this newsletter. The model fills them from training memory under pressure
and gets them wrong.

CANONICAL FAILURE: 6/1/2026 lead claimed the Knicks "ended a 53-year Finals
drought" upon reaching the 2026 Finals. The 53-year figure is the
CHAMPIONSHIP drought (last title 1973, still ongoing). The APPEARANCE drought
is 27 years (last Finals appearance 1999). Conflating the two destroyed
reader trust on the most-read line of the issue.

RULES:
1. If a specific numerical historical claim appears in a tweet or article
   provided in raw_content, you may quote it — use the exact framing in the
   source.
2. If you want to make a historical claim that is NOT in raw_content, you
   must specify what kind of drought/streak (championship vs. appearance
   vs. playoff series vs. playoff win) AND that exact number must be
   correct. If you cannot verify the kind and the number, do not make the
   claim.
3. If you must reference history without a verified number, use RELATIVE
   framing — "their first Finals since the '90s," "their longest run in a
   generation" — not specific years or counts. Relative phrasing cannot be
   factually wrong; specific phrasing can.
4. "Defending champion" applies only to the team that won the most recent
   completed championship. Verify against game_state.json before using.

The principle: a specific wrong number is far worse than a vague right
impression. When in doubt, write it vague.

RULE 4 — SENTENCE RHYTHM AND PARALLEL CONSTRUCTIONS [Added: 2026-06-01]
================================================================================
Two failures from 6/1/2026 share a root cause: the writer hits the
punchy-fragment register and never exits it. SLAP voice IS punchy, but six
consecutive short sentences reads like a Wikipedia bullet list converted to
prose, not group-chat narration.

FAILURE A — choppy parallel:
  "Victor Wembanyama is 22. In 1999, the Spurs won their first championship.
   Tim Duncan was 23 in his second season. Wemby is in his third. Duncan won
   Finals MVP that year. Wembanyama just dropped 22 and seven boards in Game
   7 to eliminate the defending champion Thunder."
Six sentences, all 5–14 words, all the same rhythm. The Wemby/Duncan
parallel is interesting content rendered as a list.

FIX A: When stacking a multi-fact parallel, vary length. One or two short
sentences for impact, then weave one longer sentence that connects the
threads. The goal is the rhythm of a friend talking, not a stat sheet.

FAILURE B — broken "or" parallelism:
  "Twenty-seven years later, they're back. Or the Knicks finally get revenge."
The first half is about an APPEARANCE ("they're back"). The second half is
about an OUTCOME ("finally get revenge"). "Or" demands both halves describe
the same dimension. Mixing dimensions reads as a non-sequitur.

FIX B: If you write a sentence with "Or X" as an alternate framing, both
halves must describe the same dimension. Either both about appearance —
"both franchises are back" — or both about outcome — "the Spurs run it back.
Or the Knicks finally get even." Never mix.

THE RULE: After writing a passage, count consecutive sentences under 15
words. If you hit four, the next sentence must be longer. After writing any
"Or X" construction, verify both halves describe the same dimension; if not,
rewrite one of them.

RULE 5 — EMPTY CLOSER FRAGMENTS [Added: 2026-08-31]
================================================================================
A recurring failure across 8/29-8/30 output: a passage ends on a short
fragment that restates timing or scale but adds no new information — a
stage direction standing in for content.

FAILURE EXAMPLES:
  "Allen is 34. On a one-year deal. Signed twelve days ago. Do the math."
  "The Colts WR depth chart without Allen and Pierce: Josh Downs, Nick
   Westbrook-Ikhine, and Laquon Treadwell. In 2026."

Both passages were flagged "starts solid, turns meh" / "ends meh" — the
setup (the facts) worked, the closer added nothing and just restated what
the reader already knows (the year, that they should do arithmetic).

THE RULE: A closing fragment must land a fact or a joke, not just gesture
at one. Before shipping a trailing one-line fragment, ask: does this add
information the reader didn't already have? If it's just naming the year,
telling the reader to do math, or otherwise pointing at the punchline
instead of being one — cut it and end on the last real fact instead.
