Don Nelson, Hall of Fame Coach, Dies at 86

Don Nelson passed away Sunday morning, surrounded by his family. He was 86 years old.

Nellie was one of the most consequential figures in the history of basketball. Five NBA championships as a player with the Celtics. Three-time Coach of the Year. The second-winningest coach in NBA history. A member of the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame. And the architect of "Nellie Ball" — the small-ball, positionless, run-and-gun style that anticipated the modern NBA by a generation.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2086518089315704891

Before spacing and pace were league-wide dogma, Nelson was running five-out lineups, playing point forwards, and letting big men handle the ball. The 2007 "We Believe" Warriors — the eighth seed that took down the 67-win Mavericks in the first round — was Nellie's system in its purest form. So was the Nash-Dirk Mavericks team that changed how the position of power forward was played.

He also had an eye for international talent when very few people in the league did. He drafted Dirk Nowitzki. He coached and championed players from Europe and beyond at a time when that was still viewed with skepticism. Both Luka Dončić and Dirk shared tributes on Sunday that spoke to that legacy.

https://twitter.com/lukadoncic/status/2086519568034615344

https://twitter.com/swish41/status/2086524774318997601

Nelson finished his coaching career with 1,335 wins. He coached the Bucks, Warriors (twice), Knicks, and Mavericks. The list of coaches and executives who came up through his tree — Avery Johnson, Mike Dunleavy Sr., Del Harris — runs long. His fingerprints are still on how the league is played in 2026.

https://twitter.com/Novig/status/2086564349762556188

Rest in peace, Coach.

Jacob Misiorowski Hits 200 Ks, Second-Fastest in MLB History

Jacob Misiorowski struck out Alan Roden of the Twins on Sunday for his 200th K of the season. He got there in 129⅓ innings — the second-fastest anyone has ever done it.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2086530236288540757

The only pitcher ahead of him is Spencer Strider, who struck out 200 hitters in 123⅓ innings in 2023. Strider basically owns this list — his 2022 season sits third at 130 IP flat. The entire ceiling of this record: Strider, Misiorowski, Strider again.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2086526082014929322

The rest of his 2026 resume: a career-best 1.76 ERA, an MLB-leading 204 total strikeouts, and 30 of 33 first-place votes in MLB's latest Cy Young poll. He is not the Cy Young favorite. He is the Cy Young.

Kayla McBride Hits 10 Threes, Drops 43, Lynx Clinch

Kayla McBride became the first player in WNBA history to make ten three-pointers in a game.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2086567176526336090

The previous single-game record was nine. It took the entire history of the league to get there. Nine three-pointers had been the WNBA's single-game ceiling since Kelsey Mitchell first reached it in 2019, with eight other players eventually matching it. McBride skipped past all of them.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2086567256457125967

She's 33 years old. She's a 13-year veteran. And she just got snubbed for the All-Star team this year while three of her Lynx teammates made it. This performance felt like a receipt.
