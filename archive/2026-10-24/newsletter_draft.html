Brewers Survive Wild 9th to Steal Game 1 — First World Series Win in Decades

One pitch away from the celebration. Then one call away from losing it. Then one replay away from getting it back.

Bases loaded. Game-ending double play. Celebration. Review. Overturned. Still bases loaded. Megill back on the mound. Milwaukee fans aged through three presidential administrations in sixty seconds.

Milwaukee fans during the replay review:

Jacob Misiorowski's first pitch of his first career World Series start: 103.5 mph. Fastest pitch in World Series history since tracking began in 2008. Previous record: 103.2 mph, Aroldis Chapman, 2016.

Then he threw 30 pitches at 101 mph or higher. Eleven at 102-plus. Three at 103-plus. Five innings, one run.

Season total: 1,115 pitches of at least 100 mph. Previous record: Jordan Hicks, 673. He didn't break the record. He doubled it.

The Rays lost being exactly who they are. Victor Mesa Jr. caught stealing third. Aggressive baserunning that worked all October finally cost them in the one game where every out matters.

Milwaukee's last World Series appearance was 1982. Reagan's first term. The last Brewer to play in a World Series game is now old enough to be a grandfather to the current roster.
