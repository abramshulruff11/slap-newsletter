Jason Collins Has Died at 47

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2054327698105704567

Jason Collins became the first openly gay player in any of the four major American sports when he came out in a 2013 Sports Illustrated essay while still active. He played 13 NBA seasons with the Nets, Wizards, Grizzlies, Timberwolves, Hawks, and Celtics. He was 47 years old.

Collins was diagnosed with Stage 4 glioblastoma in December. Glioblastoma is an aggressive brain cancer that also took John McCain, Ted Kennedy, and Beau Biden. It moves fast and leaves little time. Collins wrote an essay about his battle in December. Five months later, he's gone.

What Collins did in 2013 required courage most of us will never understand. He came out during his playing career, not after. He opened the door for every athlete who came after him — Michael Sam, Robbie Rogers, Carl Nassib, and countless others who now play without having to hide who they are. He made that possible.

The NBA lost a pioneer today. Basketball lost someone who mattered beyond the game. 47 is far too young.

Brandon Clarke Has Died at 29

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2054260698780250331

Brandon Clarke was 29 years old. He was the 21st pick in the 2019 draft out of Gonzaga and spent all seven seasons with the Memphis Grizzlies. He averaged 10.3 points and 5.8 rebounds per game for his career. He was a solid role player, an efficient scorer, and a good defender.

https://twitter.com/coleadamss/status/2054268913576182119

No cause of death has been reported. The Grizzlies organization and the NBA community are devastated. Two NBA players died on the same day. The basketball world is reeling.

Sixers Fire Daryl Morey

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2054348886957859215

The Sixers fired the GM and kept the coach. Backward. Teams fire the coach first, then let the new GM pick his guy. Philadelphia kept Nick Nurse going into his fourth season and fired Morey instead.

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2054348189462151560

Bob Myers — who built the Warriors dynasty — will lead the search for Philly's new president of basketball operations. That's the one good piece of news.
