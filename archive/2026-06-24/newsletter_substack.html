Draft Night Theater

The 2026 NBA Draft delivered exactly what a draft is supposed to deliver: a generational No. 1, a snubbed franchise, a coach drafting his own kid, and Chicago doubling down on a meme that already worked once.

AJ Dybantsa is a Washington Wizard. The BYU freshman led Division I in scoring and walks into the league with All-NBA upside attached to his scouting report in permanent marker.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2069574562166219030

BYU's first-ever No. 1 pick. The highest-drafted Cougar since Shawn Bradley went second overall in 1993, which is a sentence that should not exist about the same school but here we are. Washington now has three No. 1 picks in franchise history and exactly zero idea what to do with any of them historically — Dybantsa is the test of whether the Wizards have learned anything in thirty years.

Then it got weird at No. 2.

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2069576775672177067

Peterson reportedly only took a pre-draft visit with the Wizards. Not the Jazz. The team that just picked him second overall. He's now the highest draft pick in Utah Jazz history and they had to introduce themselves to him on a TV broadcast.

Utah's front office walking out of the green room after the pick:

Memphis took Cam Boozer at No. 3, which is the safest pick in the entire draft and possibly the entire decade.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2069577107781628094

And then Chicago got on the clock and did the thing Chicago does.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2069577917248774489

The Bulls drafted Caleb Wilson at No. 4. The Bears have Caleb Williams. Chicago is now officially running a Caleb W. cinematic universe across two pro sports franchises, and somewhere a marketing intern is being asked to design the joint billboard by sundown.

Then came the Michigan run, and it is genuinely historic.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2069646433708655065

Three lottery picks from the reigning national champ. It marks the first time since Florida in 2007 that three non-freshmen from the same school were lottery picks — that Florida team had Horford, Noah, and Brewer, won back-to-back titles, and immediately stopped being a basketball school for the next decade. No pressure, Wolverines.

Morez Johnson Jr. went to Dallas at No. 9. The Dallas Mavericks. Where Dusty May was officially introduced as head coach hours earlier the same day. The same Dusty May who coached Morez Johnson Jr. at Michigan eleven weeks ago.

https://twitter.com/coleadamss/status/2069587152036409617

https://twitter.com/BarstoolBigCat/status/2069591983119618407

First move as an NBA head coach: drafting your own recruit. Dusty May skipped the entire "build a culture, scout the league, learn the personnel" phase and went straight to "I know a guy."

"I was insanely shocked when I saw Dusty was going to be coach of the Mavs," Johnson said. Sure, Morez. Sure.

Warriors got Yaxel Lendeborg at 11. Thunder got Aday Mara at 12. The reigning champs are now coaching SGA's understudy, Steph's understudy, and Dusty May's actual former player simultaneously. Michigan basketball is currently more represented in the NBA's elite tier than most NBA teams.

Ronaldo Roars, Kane Chokes, World Cup Rolls

Cristiano Ronaldo had not scored a goal in a major tournament since November 2022. He had not scored in 10 straight major tournament matches. Portugal had drawn DR Congo 1-1 in the opener, the Portuguese press was openly questioning whether he should still be the No. 9, and the Group K stage was set for either redemption or a televised funeral.

He scored in the sixth minute. He scored again before halftime. Portugal won 5-0.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2069495858693914634

It wasn't just a brace. Ronaldo became the first player to score in six different World Cups, finding the net in Portugal's 5-0 win over Uzbekistan. His brace took his World Cup tally to 10 goals, surpassing Portuguese legend Eusébio's record of nine goals and making Ronaldo Portugal's highest scorer in World Cup history.

https://twitter.com/SleeperHQ/status/2069479732341620778

He does not. Messi is at 18 after his hat trick last week. The Messi-Ronaldo debate continues to be the only debate that refuses to die regardless of what either man does on a field.

Meanwhile, at the other end of the emotional spectrum, Harry Kane.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2069545380077781387

https://twitter.com/PatMcAfeeShow/status/2069538583883419833

England drew 0-0 with Ghana. Kane missed a sitter that English supporters will be screaming about until their grandchildren are old. Ronaldo turns 41 and ends a four-year drought; Kane is 32 and in his prime and misses a goal you could roll into with your eyes closed.

Colombia handled DR Congo 1-0 thanks to Daniel Muñoz.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2069626249178460400

Croatia beat Panama 1-0 in a game that exists. Group stage marches on, and the only takeaway from Tuesday is that Cristiano Ronaldo is one of the most stubborn men in the history of sports and he just made everyone in Portugal eat their words at 41 years old.

League Draws The Line

The NFL is not letting Brendan Sorsby in. Not this summer, not via the Supplemental Draft, not after a three-business-day application with no supporting documents. He's stuck waiting for the 2027 Draft.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2069473316935483807

The letter, obtained by Schefter, reads like a court filing. The NFL outlined Sorsby's NCAA findings — sustained gambling on his own team and teammates, using intermediary accounts to hide the bets, no demonstrated accountability — and basically said "you're a talented player, see you in 2027."

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2069478343611748532

Sorsby's attorney Jeffrey Kessler — the same Kessler who's beaten the leagues in court before — fired back. He says the NFL told them all they needed was a short application, declined the invitation to submit more, and then leaked the denial letter to the media before sending it to Sorsby himself.

https://twitter.com/TomPelissero/status/2069543729686605900

The honest read here is Albert Breer's: the NFL has let in players with drug issues, NCAA violations, and grade issues for decades. Gambling is the one thing they will not touch, because gambling is the one thing they're now in business with.

https://twitter.com/AlbertBreer/status/2069544085514223745

Lollipops, Walk-Offs, and Walk Violations

Aaron Boone banned lollipops on the field earlier this week. Jazz Chisholm Jr. hit a go-ahead two-run home run against Detroit on Tuesday night.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2069574133365580046

"No lollipop in sight" was a lie of omission. They were in the dugout. They were waiting.

https://twitter.com/JomboyMedia/status/2069578305544561027

The Yankees won 4-3. The clubhouse reportedly blasted "Lollipop" by Lil Wayne after the game. This is the most public a player has ever been about ignoring a manager's directive in modern baseball history, and the only available response is admiration.

Down in San Diego, Manny Machado decided to end Atlanta's night personally.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2069644964854681919

And in Chicago, Pete Crow-Armstrong learned a rule that nobody in the ballpark knew existed.

https://twitter.com/JomboyMedia/status/2069617585490973029

PCA walked. He kept running to second. He came off the bag. He got tagged. He's out. Welcome to baseball, where you can be punished for hustling and there are 47 different ways to be retired without anyone in the stadium understanding any of them.

Quiet ace of the night: Justin Wrobleski.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2069624777560523157

9-2, 2.71 ERA. Best Dodger starter nobody outside Los Angeles can pick out of a lineup.

Kelly's Latest Fight

Jim Kelly is one of the toughest people in NFL history and the universe keeps testing it.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2069560384667705694

Kelly has fought oral cancer, sinus cancer, and multiple recurrences over the last decade. Now he's adding a stroke to the list and telling reporters he feels "really good." The man led the Bills to four straight Super Bowls and has been the spiritual backbone of Western New York for forty years. Get well, Jim. Buffalo's not the same without you upright.
