The Rams Flew 15 Hours to Get Boat-Raced in Front of 100,000 Australians

The Rams were the preseason Super Bowl favorite. They added Myles Garrett to pair with a full defensive line. They had the reigning MVP under center. They flew to Melbourne to open the season in the first-ever NFL regular season game on Australian soil, in front of a packed Melbourne Cricket Ground.

The 49ers put 27 on them and pulled Matthew Stafford with three minutes left.

Myles Garrett Recorded Zero of Everything

Not a sack. Not a tackle. Not a QB hit. Not a pressure. 27 pass rush snaps. Zero pressures.

The Rams gave up Jared Verse — the 2024 Defensive Rookie of the Year — plus a 2027 first-round pick to acquire him. San Francisco's plan: trust Trent Williams on the blindside, run a quick short-to-intermediate passing game, and dedicate blockers to the run. Once Garrett and the Rams' front got worn down, Purdy opened up the downfield stuff. Williams took his lunch money and gave him the receipt.

Tom Pelissero, from the sideline in the fourth quarter: every time Garrett left the field, he really seemed gassed. On the sideline, cooked, down 24-7, halfway around the world.

Federal Prosecutors Are Now in the Clippers Group Chat

One week ago, the NBA stripped the Clippers of five first-round picks, fined Steve Ballmer $30 million, suspended him along with Lawrence Frank and Trent Redden, fined Kawhi $700K, and banned Uncle Dennis for five years — all over the Aspiration endorsement deal that looked a lot like circumventing the salary cap.

The Clippers said they'd fight it through every avenue. Today, federal prosecutors in Brooklyn opened a criminal investigation into the same conduct.

The league handles cap violations internally. When the feds show up, they're looking at wire fraud, tax fraud, or conspiracy — the kind of stuff that doesn't get resolved with a fine and a strongly worded press release. Losing five firsts was the ceiling. Now it's the floor.
