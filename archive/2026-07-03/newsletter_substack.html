Ronaldo Finally Scores a Knockout Goal. Ramos Steals the Show. VAR Steals a Nation's Soul.

Portugal 2, Croatia 1. Round of 32. BMO Field, Toronto. And somewhere between the 67th and 103rd minute, we got approximately four different games' worth of drama.

Cristiano Ronaldo, sitting joint-11th on the all-time World Cup scorers' list, had scored all of his prior goals in the group stage. Every single one. Going into Thursday, he had attempted 29 shots in eight World Cup knockout stage games without scoring, tied for the most of any player since 1966 alongside Brazil's Roberto Carlos.

Then, in the 68th minute, penalty. Buried it.

Twenty years. Six World Cups. One knockout goal. At 41 years and 147 days old, he became the oldest player to ever play in a World Cup knockout-stage match, and the oldest player to score a goal in the knockout stage of the World Cup.

The historical context makes this even wilder. This is the fourth comeback win in the knockout stages of this World Cup, the most in a single tournament since 1970 — the only World Cup with more comeback knockout wins is 1934 with six. This was Portugal's first comeback win at a World Cup knockout stage game since 1966 vs. North Korea. Sixty years.

One more stat that broke us: this was the first match in FIFA World Cup history to feature three different players who have appeared in more than 20 World Cup matches — Ronaldo (26), Modric (23), Ivan Perisic (21). Three fossils, one field, one final act for two of them.

Spain Cruises. Yamal's Little Brother Steals the Broadcast.

Spain 3, Austria 0. It was never a game.

With a dominant 3-0 victory over Austria, Spain won a knockout-round match at the World Cup for the first time since 2010. Mikel Oyarzabal opened the scoring in the first half before Pedro Porro extended the lead to 2-0 on a header, and Oyarzabal added the final margin in the closing minutes.

The best moment of the broadcast happened in the stands.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2072827557037187519

Meanwhile the actual star of the family, 18-year-old Lamine Yamal, became the youngest player on record since 1966 in a FIFA World Cup match to record both a goal and an assist. Pau Cubarsí and Lamine Yamal are the first pair of teenagers to start a FIFA World Cup knockout match for a team for 68 years.

Now the bracket gods have gifted us Portugal-Spain in the Round of 16. Ronaldo (41) vs. Yamal (18). A 23-year age gap on the pitch. The last time Spain and Portugal met in a World Cup knockout was 2010 — Spain won 1-0 on a David Villa goal, then went on to win the whole thing.
