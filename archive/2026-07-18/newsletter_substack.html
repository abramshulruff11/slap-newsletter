Caitlin Clark does what no one has ever done

Twenty-eight seasons of WNBA basketball. Never once had a player scored 40 points and dished 10 assists in the same game. Diana Taurasi didn't do it. Cynthia Cooper didn't do it. Sue Bird, Maya Moore, Breanna Stewart — none of them.

Caitlin Clark did it on a Friday in July. In 29 minutes.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2078293656516063637

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2078293541244338542

The Fever beat the Storm 110-107. The line is absurd on its own. 45-10-4-2 on 61% shooting in under 30 minutes is a video game slider set to "cheat mode."

The efficiency detail is the punchline. Only two players in WNBA history have scored 40+ points in fewer than 30 minutes played. Clark now holds both spots on the top of the list and the runner-up:

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2078293918895022524

The receipts kept piling up all night. Clark also became the fastest player in WNBA history to make 200 career 3-pointers, doing it in 74 games and breaking the previous mark of 81 games set by Katie Smith. Her 45 points were a franchise record too.

Storm defenders trying to figure out how to guard a player shooting 6-of-10 from three, 17-of-19 from the line, and passing for 10 assists on the same night:

The 29-minute detail matters. Coach Stephanie White wasn't running her out there for 40 minutes to pad a stat line. Clark did this on a minutes restriction coming off a back injury. She told her coach at halftime she felt really good and didn't want to lose the game. There was no way she was coming out in the fourth quarter. She'd play with one leg.

The game had a real closing sequence too. Down the stretch, tied, less than a minute left — Clark chased down a Flau'jae Johnson fast-break layup from behind, blocked it out of bounds, then jogged up the floor and hit a step-back three from 26 feet on the next possession to put Indiana up for good.

Then she went 4-for-4 from the line to seal it. A perfectly efficient closing kit tacked onto a historic scoring night. She turned a 45-10-4-2 into a game-winner.

The Red Sox cannot stop winning

Eleven straight. Doubleheader sweep. 10-0 and 5-3.

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2078298128034189797

Ten years. That's how long it's been since Boston strung together eleven wins in a row. This is a team that was 11 games under .500 when the streak started. Now they're back to even.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2078294590956683523

The July split explains why this is happening:

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2078294475558756600

They were a .500-ish team playing .400 ball for four months, then flipped a switch and started playing like the '27 Yankees. Game 1 of the doubleheader was a Jake Bennett one-hitter over six innings. Game 2 they used six relievers and gave up zero runs across 6.2 innings after a shaky start.

The franchise record is 15 straight, set in 1946. Four to go.

The Yankees cannot win when Gerrit Cole starts

The highest-paid pitcher in baseball. Nine-year, $324 million contract. Reigning Cy Young winner as recently as 2023. And the Yankees are 3-6 when he takes the mound.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2078298555488301450

Dodgers 2, Yankees 1. Max Muncy took Cole deep in the seventh with a 2-2 slider that ended up halfway up the second deck. Cole had held him hitless in five previous encounters. Just needed one swing.

And LA nearly gave the run right back. Yankees put a runner in scoring position and lined a ball to the outfield that should have tied it. Mookie Betts to Dalton Rushing:

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2078295105539129463

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2078296667573620826

Cole in 2023: 2.63 ERA, 15-4, unanimous Cy Young winner. Cole in 2026 through nine starts: 4.04 ERA, 3-4 record, 47 strikeouts in 49 innings. Not the same guy.
