Padres Walk the Double-Play Tightrope to Force Game 4

San Diego's season was six outs from over. Then Michael King walked out of the bullpen two days after starting Game 2, and the Padres won 4-3 to keep breathing.

Jake Cronenworth put San Diego back in front with a tiebreaking homer in the fifth. Manny Machado tacked on an insurance RBI. Christian Yelich singled home a run in the 7th to pull Milwaukee within one. Every lead felt like the last run anyone would score.

@SportsCenter
The Padres take Game 3 vs. the Brewers in the NLDS 👀
View tweet

King had thrown 72 pitches starting Game 2 on Sunday. Mason Miller was on paternity leave. Adrian Morejon was down with fatigue. The Padres manager asked for six outs on one day of rest.

@TalkinBaseball_
View tweet

Only pitcher since saves became an official stat in 1969 to start a postseason game and then record a save of at least six outs. First save for King in years.

In between the heroics, this game was decided by gloves. Jackson Merrill made highlight after highlight in center. Mauricio Dubón robbed Freddie Freeman of extra bases over on the Dodgers side of the night, and Milwaukee had its own leather on display. Then Cooper Pratt threw one away on a wonky play and an insurance run scored.

Then the ABS mess. Samad Taylor tapped his helmet — the universal "I'm challenging" signal — and the umpires just… didn't make him do it. Milwaukee's bench went nuclear.

Then the 9th. King struck out Jake Bauers, then walked William Contreras after a nine-pitch battle, then walked Brice Turang. Tying run on second. Go-ahead run on first. Christian Yelich at the plate. Joe Musgrove and Robbie Ray warming up in a bullpen that had nothing left.

Yelich turned on an 84 mph slider. 107 mph off the bat. Right at Xander Bogaerts, who stepped on second and fired to Ty France.

Padres live. Game 4 tonight at Petco. Series 2-1 Brewers.

Milwaukee has now lost its last dozen postseason road games.

Lane Johnson Walks Away: "I Owe It to My Mental Health"

@AdamSchefter
Eagles perennial Pro Bowl OT Lane Johnson announced his retirement.
View tweet

Six Pro Bowls. Five All-Pros. Two Super Bowls. Fourteen seasons, all in Philadelphia. And a statement that said the quiet part out loud: he needs to focus on his mental health, and he owes it to his teammates to have his head and his heart 100% in the game.

That's a rare thing from a player who could still start on Sunday.

Johnson arrived at junior college as a quarterback, got to Oklahoma as a quarterback, grew into a tight end, grew into a right tackle, and became arguably the best right tackle of his generation. From a 2013 first round that produced Eric Fisher, Luke Joeckel, Dion Jordan, and a lot of regret, Johnson was the only star.
