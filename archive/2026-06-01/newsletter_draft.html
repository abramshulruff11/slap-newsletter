Raymond Berry, Patriots Super Bowl XX Coach and Colts Hall of Fame Receiver, Dies

Raymond Berry played as a split end for the Baltimore Colts from 1955 to 1967. With the Colts, Berry led the NFL in receptions and receiving yards three times and in receiving touchdowns twice, and was invited to six Pro Bowls. The Colts won consecutive NFL championships, including the 1958 NFL Championship Game—known as "The Greatest Game Ever Played"—in which Berry caught 12 passes for 178 yards and a touchdown.

Post-retirement, Berry transitioned into coaching, serving as head coach of the New England Patriots from 1984 to 1989, leading them to Super Bowl XX. The Bears defeated the Patriots by the score of 46–10 in Super Bowl XX on January 26, 1986.

@AlbertBreer
Very sad. Unbelievable player at receiver for the Johnny Unitas Colts, and then a Super Bowl coach for the Patriots. One of my earliest memories as a football fan growing up outside Boston is the "Berry the Bears" Super Bowl t-shirts from January '86. RIP Raymond Berry.
View tweet

"Berry the Bears." That was the rallying cry in Boston in January 1986. The Patriots were going to the Super Bowl for the first time in franchise history. Berry, the Hall of Fame receiver turned head coach, was taking them there. The shirts were everywhere.

The Bears won 46-10. But Berry got them to the mountain. The Patriots beat the New York Jets 26–14, Los Angeles Raiders 27–20, and the Dolphins 31–14 – all on the road – to make it to the Super Bowl. The win against Miami had been especially surprising, not only because Miami was the only team to beat Chicago in the season, but also because New England had not won in the Orange Bowl since 1966.

Berry retired as the all-time NFL leader in both receptions and receiving yardage. His subsequent rise to the Pro Football Hall of Fame has been touted as one of football's cinderella stories. Drafted in the 20th round in 1954, considered a long shot to even make the roster. Became one of the greatest receivers of all time. Then coached a franchise to its first Super Bowl.
