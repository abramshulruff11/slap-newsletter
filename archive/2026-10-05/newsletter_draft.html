Chourio Walks Off The Reaper, Brewers One Win From the NLCS

Bottom of the ninth. Two outs. Bases loaded. Mason Miller trying to steal a six-out save in Milwaukee. The 22-year-old steps in.

101 off Miller's hand. 110 off Chourio's bat. Two-RBI single, game over, Brewers up 2-0.

Miller struck out 49% of the batters he faced this year. Of the 104 batters he faced early in the season, 51 struck out and the 12 hits allowed were all singles. He finished with a 1.67 ERA and 129 strikeouts, the most ever by a Padres reliever. That's the guy Chourio ambushed.

He's 22. Per Sarah Langs, only Edgar Renteria has ever been younger with a walk-off hit in postseason history — twice, in the 1997 NLDS and the 1997 World Series. Chourio is now the third name on that list.

He's also already the Brewers' all-time postseason home run leader, passing Prince Fielder and Orlando Arcia, and tied with Kyle Schwarber and Juan Soto for the most postseason home runs before turning 23.

Miller watched it from the side of the mound and started a slow walk to the visiting clubhouse. He'd been asked for the last six outs. He got five.

Milwaukee is one win from the NLCS. Game 3 is Tuesday at Petco, where the Padres now have to win three straight.
