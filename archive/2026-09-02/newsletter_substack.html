Arte Moreno Sells the Angels to Stan Kroenke for $4 Billion

Twenty-three years of Arte Moreno owning the Angels ended Tuesday with a check the size of a small country's GDP.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2094857246907736450

Four billion dollars. Crushed the Padres' $3.9 billion record and Steve Cohen's $2.4 billion Mets purchase. Kroenke now owns the reigning Super Bowl-winning Rams, the 2025-champion Nuggets, Arsenal, the Avalanche, the Rapids, the Mammoth, and a baseball team in the second-biggest market in the sport.

Kroenke just paid a record price for the worst-run asset in baseball. The Angels hold the longest active postseason drought in MLB — 11 seasons, since 2014. Three years longer than the Pirates. They employed Mike Trout and Shohei Ohtani simultaneously and made zero playoff appearances with either of them. Ohtani left, won a title in Los Angeles wearing a different shade of blue, and the Angels stayed home.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2095030492886147207

Fans chanting "goodbye Arte" at a ballgame is not normal. That's a fanbase that spent a decade watching Moreno sign Albert Pujols to a deal that ended in a DFA, Josh Hamilton to $125 million for two productive months, and Anthony Rendon to $245 million for a highlight reel of press conferences about ranking baseball fifth in his life priorities.

Moreno walks away with the second-best sports investment return of the century. Paid $183.5 million for the team in 2003. Sold for over $4 billion twenty-three years later. 2,000% return on the worst-run franchise in baseball.

The lesson: you don't have to be good, you just have to hold.
