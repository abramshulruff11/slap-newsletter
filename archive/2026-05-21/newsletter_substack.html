SGA SILENCES THE HATERS

The internet spent 48 hours roasting Shai Gilgeous-Alexander. 15 points in Game 1. Worst playoff performance of his career. Twitter was merciless. The takes were flying. The slander was relentless.

Wednesday night, the back-to-back MVP walked into his building and reminded everyone exactly who he is.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2057296837631942738

30 points, 9 assists, only 1 turnover. Thunder defend home court, series tied 1-1. Oklahoma City forced 21 turnovers and turned Stephon Castle's Game 2 into a nightmare. But the story of the night was SGA's response and Isaiah Hartenstein's assignment.

Hartenstein played the entire 4th quarter — 12 straight minutes after getting just 12 minutes total in Game 1. His line: 10 points, 13 rebounds, 8 offensive boards. But the real job was bodying Victor Wembanyama. Wemby finished with 21-17-6-4 blocks, but just 4 points in the 4th. Hartenstein held him without a bucket when it mattered most.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2057296936248451130

SGA postgame, asked about Hartenstein's defense on Wemby: "I'm not sure if it was good to be honest. Yeah… It was alright, it was good."

Translation: Hartenstein was just fouling him all game. And he was. The video evidence is everywhere. Grabbing arms, pulling hair, bodying him off every cut. The refs let it ride.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2057298709038432274

The Jared McCain moment: SGA hit a dagger jumper late, told the rookie to "calm the f**k down" mid-shot. Postgame quote: "Jared was yelling at me while I was shooting and I was just like bro I'm shooting. Don't distract me." McCain is a rookie getting playoff minutes for the defending champs. That's how deep this Thunder roster is.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2057314125223461155

Alex Caruso off the bench: 17 points, 5 assists, 5-7 shooting. Captain Chaos showed up when the champs needed depth scoring. Jalen Williams (hamstring tightness) didn't return after the first half, but Oklahoma City didn't flinch.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2057296890954228149

The Spurs are already banged up. Dylan Harper left in the 2nd half with a right leg injury. De'Aaron Fox is still out. Stephon Castle is being asked to do everything, and Wednesday night he crumbled under the pressure — 9 turnovers, 20 total through two games. But we'll get to that.

Series tied 1-1. Shifts to San Antonio for Game 3. This is going 7.

CASTLE'S BEAUTIFUL CHAOS

Stephon Castle is making history in two opposite directions at the same time.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2057305481287221568

20 turnovers through two Conference Finals games. The most in NBA history over a two-game CF stretch. Worse than every young player who ever got thrown into the fire. Worse than LeBron in 2007, worse than Magic's rookie Finals, worse than anyone.

And yet.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2057296974383120431

25 points, 5 rebounds, 8 assists. Youngest player in NBA history with 25/5/5 in a Conference Finals game. In Game 1, he became the youngest ever with 30/10/5/5 in a playoff game. He's creating everything for the Spurs with Fox and Harper out. He's a 21-year-old being asked to run the offense against the defending champion Thunder. Every possession runs through him. The mistakes are brutal. The production is undeniable.

And then there's this:

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2057297384283971983

Castle threw down a poster on Isaiah Hartenstein in the 2nd quarter that left Wemby and SGA's jaws on the floor. One of the dunks of the year. Fearless. Then Hartenstein, frustrated after getting baptized, grabbed Castle by the hair and pulled him down on a separate play in the 4th. No call. Spurs fans lost their minds.

https://twitter.com/TheNBACentel/status/2057269930626601236

The Spurs are 1-1 despite Castle turning it over 20 times. That tells you how much they need him. He's not coming off the floor. This is what learning looks like at the highest level.

VEGAS STEALS GAME 1 WITHOUT MAKAR

Cale Makar is LeBron-level important to the Avalanche. Coach Jared Bednar announced before Game 1 that Makar (day-to-day, undisclosed injury) would miss the opener. Colorado's best player, their engine, the guy who runs everything — sidelined for the Western Conference Finals.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2057294317140553985

Vegas took full advantage. 4-2 win in Denver. Dylan Coghlan scored his first career playoff goal. Carter Hart made 36 saves. Mitch Marner fed Pavel Dorofeyev for an insurance goal. The Golden Knights have home-ice advantage now — they stole Game 1 on the road, and Game 2 is still in Denver before the series shifts to Vegas.

Colorado had moments. Valeri Nichushkin scored a between-the-legs beauty. Nathan MacKinnon extended his goal streak. But without Makar running the blue line and quarterbacking the power play, they couldn't sustain offense.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2057288865421906261

The question: How long is Makar out? Bednar said "day-to-day" which in playoff speak means anything. If he's back for Game 2, Avs are fine. If this lingers, they're in trouble. No team can afford to lose their best player in the Conference Finals.

AARON RODGERS: ONE LAST RIDE

Aaron Rodgers made it official at Steelers OTAs Wednesday: "This is it." 22nd and final NFL season. He'll turn 43 in December.

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2057169678925996379

The schedule tells the story. The NFL gave Pittsburgh the #3 easiest schedule through Week 8 to keep Rodgers competitive and in primetime. After Week 8, he's in primetime 4 of the next 5 weeks. The league is milking the farewell tour for every rating point.

https://twitter.com/SharpFootball/status/2057208078437580921

But they're also throwing him into the fire. Rodgers faces the #3 toughest schedule of pass defenses: Texans (#1 last year), Browns twice (#2), Eagles (#3), Broncos (#4), plus Ravens and Bengals with improved units. This isn't a victory lap. This is a final test.

https://twitter.com/SharpFootball/status/2057168325063344276

Rodgers needs just 13 passing TDs to pass Drew Brees for 3rd all-time (540). That's the realistic goal. Can a 42-year-old do it against this gauntlet? The Mike McCarthy reunion gives him a chance. They won a Super Bowl together in Green Bay. Now McCarthy is trying to squeeze one more playoff run out of Rodgers before he hangs it up.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2057165829519622496

135 MPH IN A 70

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2057391034972176736

COACH O IS BACK

Ed Orgeron is back at LSU. Seven years after leading the Tigers to the 2019 national championship as head coach, Coach O returns as Lane Kiffin's special assistant to recruiting and defense.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2057283652459868548

Orgeron and Kiffin have worked together before — at USC, at Tennessee. They've got a decades-long friendship. Kiffin took over at LSU this offseason and immediately brought in his old friend. Orgeron was fired by LSU in 2021 despite winning a national title two years earlier. Now he's back in a different role.

Pardon My Take was at LSU for Grit Week this week and interviewed both Kiffin and Orgeron. Big Cat and PFT were there for the moment Coach O returned to Baton Rouge.

https://twitter.com/BarstoolBigCat/status/2057284142576853157

https://twitter.com/BarstoolBigCat/status/2057318293560545785

Coach O's voice is back on the LSU recruiting trail. GEAUX TIGAHS. This is a feel-good story. A redemption arc. Orgeron led LSU to a perfect 15-0 season and the 2019 title, got fired two years later, and now he's back helping Kiffin rebuild the program. The circle is complete.
