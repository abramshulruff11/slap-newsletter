Yankees Radio Icon John Sterling Dies at 87

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2051284884090409386

John Sterling, the legendary New York Yankees radio announcer, has died. He was 87.

Sterling started as the Yankees' play-by-play announcer in 1989. Over the first 30 years of his career, he never missed a game. 5,000 straight broadcasts until 2019.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2051277708986892419

Sterling's most famous phrase: "The Yankees win." He personalized home-run calls for Yankees players during his 36 years on the job. "An A-bomb for A-Rod" for Alex Rodriguez. "Robbie Cano, don't ya know" for Robinson Cano.

https://twitter.com/PatMcAfeeShow/status/2051281780192198803

Pat McAfee said it perfectly. The only thing as great as being at Yankee Stadium was hearing Sterling call "THEEEEE YANKEES WIN" on the radio.

The Yankees won five World Series during Sterling's career. He called 5,631 total Yankees games, including 211 postseason broadcasts.

Sterling was imperfect. He'd call home runs on fly balls that didn't leave the park. He'd mispronounce names. He was a walking blooper reel. Yankees fans loved him anyway.

Thirty-six seasons. 5,631 games. One voice.
