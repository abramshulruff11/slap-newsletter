Kyle Busch, 1985-2026

Kyle Busch, a two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion and one of the sport's greatest drivers, died Thursday. He was 41.

@NASCAR
We are saddened and heartbroken to share the news of the passing of Kyle Busch, a two-time Cup champion and one of our sport's greatest and fiercest drivers. He was 41 years old.

We extend our deepest condolences to the Busch family, Richard Childress Racing and the entire motorsports community.
View tweet

Busch had been hospitalized with a severe illness. No cause of death was given.

Busch was in his 22nd full-time season in NASCAR's top division, where he won two Cup Series titles (2015, 2019) and 63 races — a figure that ranks ninth on the circuit's all-time win list. His 234 NASCAR races across NASCAR's top three series are more than any driver in history. He holds the all-time wins record in the O'Reilly Auto Parts Series (102) and Craftsman Truck Series (69).

Busch was a polarizing figure among fans — intensely popular for his supporters and booed loudly by his detractors. He entered the sport as a brash teenager with the nickname "Shrub" as the younger brother to Hall of Famer Kurt Busch, but the alias of "Rowdy" — a nod to one of the main characters in "Days of Thunder" and to his aggressive style — is what stuck with him.

"You take whatever you can get, man. You never know when the last one is going to be, so cherish them all — trust me."

Busch is certain to be a first ballot NASCAR Hall of Famer. In a Cup Series career that spanned over 20 years, Busch won two Cup Series championships (2015, 2019) and 63 races.

He is survived by his wife Samantha and children Brexton and Lennix.
