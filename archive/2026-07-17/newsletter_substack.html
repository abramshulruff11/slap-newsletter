MLB Returns From The Break Through A Wall Of Smoke

Baseball came back from the All-Star break last night. The bigger story was that you could barely see it happen.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2077893410111782919

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2077903810404462750

Canadian wildfire smoke rolled down the eastern seaboard and parked itself directly over Citizens Bank Park. MLB moved first pitch up an hour, from 7:10 to 6:10 PM ET, and had Buster Olney basically live-tweeting the air quality index between innings.

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2077878579300319356

The Chicago version of this same smoke cloud already cancelled a beach volleyball playoff match earlier in the day. This is the same haze that shut down MLB games back in June 2023 too — this is becoming a recurring summer character in the sport.

Somewhere inside the orange soup, Francisco Alvarez decided he was going to hit two home runs. Mets 4, Phillies 1. First pitch of the second half and the Mets stole one in hostile territory.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2077907773216501785

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2077921155835420951

The trade deadline is two weeks away. The Mets took the opener in Philly. Nobody could really see it. Welcome to the second half.

The World Cup Final We Wanted

Sunday in East Rutherford. Spain plays Argentina in the FIFA World Cup 2026 Final after Spain beat France 2-0 and Argentina beat England 2-1 in the semifinals. Messi versus Yamal. 39 versus 19. The old king versus the kid whose ceiling is currently classified.

The reason Argentina is even in this game is the same reason every Argentina story ends the same way for 20 years.

https://twitter.com/AlbertBreer/status/2077746920122884423

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2077826639149314475

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2077826188567794158

Messi is two assists from the all-time World Cup assist record. He'll be 42 by the time the next one rolls around in 2030. This is the last dance, and the setup is almost unfair — the guy on the other sideline is a 19-year-old who Messi once literally held as a baby in a 2007 Barcelona charity calendar shoot.

Yamal has been the story of this tournament and the run-up to it. Instagram posts predicting the France win, on-field arrogance backed up by production. He's currently the youngest player ever to appear in a World Cup final.

Also somehow the funniest stat in the tournament belongs to a team not in this final:

Cape Verde: population 500,000. Undefeated in regulation against the two teams playing for the trophy on Sunday. Someone hand them the honorary co-champion belt right now.

Scheffler Is Defending At The One Course That Actually Matters

Round 2 at Royal Birkdale is underway. Scottie Scheffler is chasing back-to-back Claret Jugs, which sounds like a standard "world No. 1 is favored" storyline until you look up who did it last and where.

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2077813947114074414

Tirico says Scheffler isn't hitting it great this year. Scheffler is currently first on the leaderboard at a major. Sure.

Scheffler is defending the Claret Jug at Royal Birkdale — the same links where Padraig Harrington pulled off golf's last successful Open defense in 2008. Not a coincidence in the storyline. Nobody has won back-to-back Opens since Harrington in 2008. Eighteen years. If Scheffler does it here — at THE course where the last guy did it — that's a plaque-on-the-wall level narrative.

Rory McIlroy meanwhile struggled in Round 1. Coming home to the UK after finally completing the career grand slam at Augusta and immediately shanking it into the gorse. British sports pain is a universal language.
