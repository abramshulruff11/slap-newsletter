Kyle Busch, 1985–2026

Kyle Busch, a two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion and one of the sport's greatest drivers, died Thursday at age 41. The Busch family released a statement Saturday morning: severe pneumonia progressed into sepsis, resulting in rapid and overwhelming associated complications. NASCAR driver Kyle Busch experienced shortness of breath, felt he was overheating and was coughing up blood the day before his death, according to a 911 call obtained Friday by The Associated Press.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2058222424701694050

Busch was the 2009 NASCAR Nationwide Series champion and a two-time NASCAR Cup Series champion, winning titles in 2015 and 2019. He won 63 Cup Series races, placing him ninth on the all-time wins list. Across NASCAR's top three national series, Busch won a combined 234 races — a record high. Born May 2, 1985, Kyle Thomas Busch came from a racing family in Las Vegas. His father, Tom, was a mechanic who raced locally. His brother, Kurt, was seven years older and set a competitive benchmark for him to aspire to on the track. Known as "Rowdy" and "Wild Thing" for his postrace fights, regular feuds with other drivers and sometimes outlandish behavior, the multitalented and polarizing Busch stormed onto the Cup Series scene in 2005 by winning Rookie of the Year. His last win in NASCAR was in the 2026 Ecosave 200, a Truck Series race at Dover Motor Speedway, six days before his death. NASCAR lost a giant of the sport today, far too soon.

Gerrit Cole Is Back, and He Looked Like He Never Left

For the first time in 569 days, Gerrit Cole was on the mound for the New York Yankees on Friday. Cole was masterful in his return from Tommy John surgery, holding the AL-best Tampa Bay Rays scoreless across six innings. He allowed two singles and three walks, struck out a pair, and threw an economical 72 pitches. His fastball averaged 96.1 mph and he topped out at 98.6 mph. The Yankees lost 4-2 behind a Rays bullpen implosion, but Cole's return was everything New York needed to see. Six shutout innings. 72 pitches. A four-pitch fourth inning. He picked up right where he left off in the 2024 World Series.
