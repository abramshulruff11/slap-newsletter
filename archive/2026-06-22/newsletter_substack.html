Wyndham Clark Beats Shinnecock, Sam Burns, and Half of Long Island to Win a Second U.S. Open

Wyndham Clark woke up Sunday with a six-shot lead and a hostile gallery. He went to bed a two-time U.S. Open champion.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2068828433489019166

Clark became the ninth wire-to-wire winner of the U.S. Open. First since Martin Kaymer in 2014 at Pinehurst. He's also the 24th multiple U.S. Open champ. The other names on that wire-to-wire list: Hagen, Barnes, Hogan, Jacklin, Tiger (twice), McIlroy, Kaymer. Wyndham Clark just elbowed his way into that room in a Jack Hughes hockey sweater.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2068828274831089918

Burns shot 3-under 67 while Clark fought to a 3-over 73. Two back-nine double bogeys. By the 17th it was a one-stroke knife fight.

https://twitter.com/BarstoolBigCat/status/2068821400320667990

And the gallery. Long Island showed up and decided Wyndham Clark was the villain.

https://twitter.com/PFTCommenter/status/2068776033604510069

https://twitter.com/BarstoolBigCat/status/2068800825128874464

Booing a birdie at a U.S. Open. New frontier. Some of that was Scheffler in the final pairing — the crowd sang him happy birthday on the first tee for his 30th — but most of it was just a guy from Denver getting the full New York treatment.

Burns made it real. Three birdies in his first five holes. By the back nine he was tied. After collapsing as the 54-hole leader a year ago at Oakmont, he shot 67 under pressure but missed his final two birdie chances that could have forced a playoff.

https://twitter.com/PatMcAfeeShow/status/2068821292329939138

Then came the part that broke everyone.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2068831321938407430

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2068891402734338256

A red-eye on Father's Day to surprise his son at the 18th green of a U.S. Open his son just won wire-to-wire. No screenwriter would pitch this.

OKC Saves $61 Million by Sending Aaron Wiggins to Atlanta

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2068883672892133822

A No. 55 pick from 2021 who developed into a championship rotation player goes to Atlanta for two seconds. The number underneath: OKC's projected luxury tax bill drops from $213M to $152M. A $61 million swing.

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2068883672892133822

The Thunder now have two picks in the top 17 of Tuesday's draft and $61M less in tax penalties. The new CBA is doing exactly what the league designed: forcing the defending champion to actively shed parts of the team that won the title. Atlanta gets a real player for nothing.

Egypt Wins a World Cup Game for the First Time in History

Egypt has played in the World Cup four times: 1934, 1990, 2018, and 2026. Across the first three trips they went 0-3-5. Nine matches. Zero wins. Today, in match number ten, Mo Salah finally did the thing.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2068884695849251185

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2068884570158645532

Egypt trailed New Zealand 1-0 early. Then Ziko equalized, Salah put them ahead, and Trezeguet headed in the dagger.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2068888314044670383

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2068893261893095472

Salah is 34. This was almost certainly his last World Cup. He delivered the most important goal in his country's footballing history on the way out the door.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2068892980325360083
