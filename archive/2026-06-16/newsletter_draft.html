Quick recap of how we got here. Sorsby admitted to placing 40+ bets on his own Indiana team. The NCAA, in a rare moment of clarity, said you cannot bet on your own games and then play in our games. A Texas judge said actually you can. The Big 12 sued. Texas Tech released a 21-minute defense video. The Texas AG threatened a $200 million antitrust suit. And after all of that — every lawyer billing hours, every conference filing paperwork, every judge weighing in — Sorsby just shrugged and said he'll see himself out.

The last time a player was taken in the supplemental draft was 2019, when the Cardinals selected Jalen Thompson in the fifth round. Before that, you have to scroll past names like Josh Gordon and Terrelle Pryor to find anyone notable. The last first-round supplemental pick was quarterback Dave Brown by the New York Giants in 1992.

1992. Bill Clinton hadn't been elected yet. If Sorsby goes in the first round, he'd be the first supplemental first-rounder since before half the league's current GMs had a driver's license.

GIF: Eduardo Saverin "a million dollars isn't cool" — each layer of this story is more absurd than the last

The all-time supplemental Mount Rushmore is genuinely insane to read. Bernie Kosar went first overall to the Browns in 1985 after leaving Miami early. Brian Bosworth went first overall to the Seahawks in 1987 following NCAA sanctions against Oklahoma. Cris Carter was a fourth-round selection in 1987 and went to the Hall of Fame. Sorsby is about to join a club that has been collecting dust for a generation.

@AdamSchefter
Everything there is to know at this time about quarterback Brendan Sorsby entering the NFL's supplemental draft:

https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/49074711/2026-nfl-supplemental-draft-brendan-sorsby-quarterback-gambling-eligibility
View tweet

The mechanics here matter. A supplemental pick costs a team the corresponding pick in next year's actual draft. So if a QB-desperate team — your Browns, your Raiders, your Saints — burns a 2027 first-rounder on Sorsby, they're betting a first-rounder in next spring's draft that a guy who got 35 days of inpatient gambling rehab is their long-term answer at quarterback.

The cleanest possible ending to the messiest possible story. The NCAA can't ban Sorsby for betting on his own team. The NFL can absolutely draft him for it.
