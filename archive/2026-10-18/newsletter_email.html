Five of the Top Seven Just Lost. In One Day. For the First Time Ever.

The AP Poll has existed since 1936. Ninety seasons. In none of them had five of the top seven teams lost on the same day. Until Saturday.

No. 1 Georgia, No. 2 Notre Dame, No. 5 Indiana, No. 6 Alabama, No. 7 Texas. The last time anything close to this happened was January 2, 1989, when five of the top eight lost. Reagan was still president.

Alex McPherson walked onto the field at Sanford Stadium and kicked a 34-yard field goal as time expired to beat No. 1 Georgia 27-24. Two years ago McPherson had his large intestine removed because of ulcerative colitis. Saturday he ended a long Georgia winning streak against unranked opponents.

In Provo, Bear Bachmeier threw for 224, ran for two touchdowns, and caught his first career receiving TD. BYU 28, Notre Dame 24 — the Cougars' first-ever win in an AP top-10 matchup. CJ Carr fumbled a spike attempt earlier in the drive. BYU recovered.

The Big 12 looked at the tape and said yeah, actually, Notre Dame should have had one more play. The clock showed :01. Officials ended the game. More on that below.

Tennessee beat Alabama 26-10 at Neyland for the third straight time. Alabama's second loss in a row.

Florida — unranked, coming off back-to-back losses — went to Austin and beat No. 7 Texas 27-24. Two straight weeks the Longhorns have fallen. Oklahoma got them last Saturday, when Texas was No. 1.

Ohio State was the only top-seven team that was guaranteed to lose a top-seven team — they played No. 5 Indiana. The Buckeyes won 19-15 after Nick Marsh stepped out of bounds on what would have been the go-ahead touchdown. Indiana had its 22-game win streak ended by a toe on a sideline.

Julian Sayin is reportedly out "several weeks" after suffering an injury. Luke Fahey will take over for the team that just climbed to the top of the poll.

Big 12 Admits Notre Dame Was Robbed of One Last Snap

The Big 12 confirmed what every Notre Dame fan was screaming at a television: the clock still had one second left when the officials ran off the field after BYU's win.

Replay backed it up. The clock never ticks to 0:00. There was also no call on what looked like pass interference on the previous play. BYU still wins — that's not getting reversed — but the league office quietly admitting the game should not have ended when it ended is the kind of thing that lives rent-free in CFP committee rooms.

Holly Rowe deserves hazard pay for whatever this was.

Miami Survives Florida State After Refs Erase Two Touchdowns in a Row

Miami beat Florida State 43-35 in double overtime. FSU scored twice in OT and neither touchdown counted.

First a Duce Robinson catch for a game-tying score — brought back on holding, on fourth down. Then a Robinson grab in the end zone — ruled a Miami interception, even though an FSU player had both hands on the ball while down and the ball ended up in a Hurricane's lap. Simultaneous possession is supposed to go to the receiver. It did not.

ESPN released the behind-the-scenes audio from the ACC Game Day Operations Center during the review of the final play. You can hear them talking themselves into the call in real time.

Valkyries Steal Game 1 Behind Gabby Williams's 26-Point Second Half

Gabby Williams had zero points at halftime. She scored 26 in the second half. Golden State 68, Atlanta 64.

Williams went 6-for-6 from the free throw line in the fourth to ice it. Kiah Stokes had four blocks, including a game-sealing swat on Angel Reese. The Valkyries shot 32.1% as a team and still won — defense and the free throw line.

Reese joined Maya Moore as the only players under 25 with a WNBA Finals double-double since 2010. Atlanta — on a 10-game win streak, undefeated since August — is now staring down a familiar hole.
