Mexico Wins a Knockout Game for the First Time in 40 Years

Julián Quiñones in the 22nd. Raúl Jiménez in the 31st. Ecuador never had a pulse. Mexico 2, Ecuador 0 at Azteca, and El Tri wins a World Cup knockout game for the first time since June 15, 1986, against Bulgaria.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2072144464903385546

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2072146630586036657

Mexico had entered the knockout rounds eight straight tournaments and gone home eight straight times. That's the longest such streak in World Cup history — a curse with a name in Spanish, the quinto partido, and an entire generation of Mexican fans who had never seen this happen in their adult lives.

The last time it did happen, the ball was leather, the anthem was different, and Diego Maradona was about to score the Hand of God two rounds later.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2072171493409411421

One more wrinkle. Ecuador's Piero Hincapié got sent off in stoppage time for covering his mouth during a chirp with Santi Gimenez — the Prestianni Rule finally biting a second victim after FIFA installed it. Yes, the actual rule is: hand over mouth, straight red. Yes, it looks insane. Yes, they enforced it.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2072168465553829958

Next up: the winner of England-DR Congo, back at Azteca in the Round of 16. England hasn't played a competitive match at that stadium in decades and their reward for winning a knockout game is a trip to 7,200 feet of elevation and 87,000 people who have been waiting 40 years to yell at them.
