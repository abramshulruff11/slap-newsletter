Pete Alonso Hits 300 at Citi Field, and Mets Fans Chant "Fire Stearns" During His Curtain Call

Pete Alonso came back to Citi Field for the first time as a visitor and hit his 300th career home run. A ninth-inning, game-tying solo shot off Kodai Senga. In the ballpark he was supposed to spend his career in. Against the team that let him walk.

19th active player to reach 300. Only Ralph Kiner and Albert Pujols hit more home runs over their first eight MLB seasons. Baltimore gave him five years, $155 million last December. His 36th of the year.

Then it got weird. Alonso's own former fanbase gave an opposing player a standing ovation and a curtain call — in a tie game, in the ninth inning, with the Mets still on the field. And while he was tipping his cap, the chant rolling around Citi Field wasn't his name. It was "FIRE STEARNS."

Even Mets pitcher Sean Manaea couldn't process it. He stood at his locker after the game and basically admitted his own fans had turned on the front office in real time.

David Stearns spent the winter deciding Alonso wasn't worth the money. Alonso just became one of the few players in league history to hit his 300th homer against his former team. The Orioles won 7-5 in 11 innings. New York got eliminated from playoff contention the night before. The fans aren't booing the visitors anymore — they're booing the guys who signed their checks.

Junior Caminero Hits Another Walk-Off, Now Leads the AL in Homers, Now Coming For Yordan Alvarez's MVP

Second walk-off homer in five days for Junior Caminero. The first one clinched the Rays' playoff berth. This one beat the A's 2-1. In between, he passed Yordan Alvarez for the AL home run lead at 41.

Jeff Passan basically wrote his MVP case for him:

- Two walk-off homers in five days
- AL home run leader at 41
- On a Rays team that just clinched a playoff spot
- Playing his best baseball in September

Yordan Alvarez had this thing locked up a month ago.

Guardians Walk Off the White Sox, Pull Within a Half Game of First

The AL Central race that nobody saw coming just got a full game tighter. The Guardians walked off the White Sox 7-6. Cleveland is now a half game back of first place. Chicago has to defend its division lead on ESPN tomorrow at 1 PM.

The Bazzana game deserves its own paragraph. Two home runs earlier in the night. Then the walk-off double in the bottom of the ninth. That walk-off hit had an expected batting average of .060. Sixty points. He got jammed, bloop found grass, game over.

Two bombs and a lucky bloop for the win. Baseball owes nobody an explanation.

Bedard is 21 years and 60 days old. The second-youngest captain in franchise history. The only player younger when he got the C was Jonathan Toews — who went on to win three Stanley Cups in five years. No pressure.

Bedard just signed a five-year, $75M extension this summer, and he's already put up 203 points in 219 games. Chicago is handing the entire rebuild to a kid who was in high school four years ago.
