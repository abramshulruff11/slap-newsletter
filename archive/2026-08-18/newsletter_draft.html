O.J. Brigance, Ravens Super Bowl Champion and ALS Warrior, Dies at 56

O.J. Brigance has died at 56.

Brigance, who won a Super Bowl with Baltimore in 2000, served as an inspiration to the organization for nearly two decades with his determination to fight the disease and surpass the initial prognosis. Diagnosed with ALS in 2007 at the age of 37, Brigance was told he had two to five years to live. But Brigance seemed undeterred, constantly smiling whether he was moving through the hallways of the team facility in a motorized wheelchair or attending games and practices. He lived nearly two decades past that prognosis.

Brigance, a Houston native, originally landed in Baltimore with the Canadian Football League's Stallions and helped the team win the 1995 Grey Cup championship. He later played linebacker and was a core special teams player on the Ravens' Super Bowl-winning team in 2000. Brigance set the tone with the first tackle of Super Bowl XXXV. He is the only player to win a Super Bowl and a Grey Cup for the same city. In 1995, he won the Grey Cup with the Baltimore Stallions.

His seven-year NFL career ended following the 2002 season, but he returned to the Ravens in a front-office role in player engagement. Brigance was recognized by the NFL, earning the Best Overall Player Development Program award for two consecutive years (2005-06). Not long after his diagnosis, he and his wife, Chanda, formed the Brigance Brigade Foundation, an organization whose mission is to "equip, encourage and empower" those afflicted with ALS.

"O.J. inspired us with humility, faithfulness, determination, competitiveness and love for humankind," Ravens general manager Eric DeCosta said. "We never once saw O.J. angry or frustrated by his disease. Instead, we all witnessed a hero who influenced so many people through his unwavering optimism and grit. O.J. Brigance is the definition of what it means to live like a Raven."

"You're my greatest motivation," Ray Lewis told Brigance after Baltimore won the AFC title in 2013. He is survived by his wife, Chanda, and the Brigance Brigade Foundation, which continues its work today.
