Tommy John, 1974 Surgery Pioneer, Dies at 83

Tommy John died Saturday night at his home in Bradenton, Florida. He was 83.

In July 1974, John — then a 31-year-old Dodgers left-hander — felt something pop in his elbow during a start against the Expos. The diagnosis was a torn ulnar collateral ligament, which at the time was a career-ending injury. Every pitcher who suffered it before John had been sent home to rest, take painkillers, and accept it was over.

John chose differently. On September 25, 1974, Dodgers team physician Dr. Frank Jobe performed an experimental procedure — taking a tendon from John's forearm and grafting it into his elbow in a figure-eight design. Jobe said in 2013 that he gave the procedure "about a one in 100 chance" of working at the time.

It worked. John, nicknamed "The Bionic Man," went on to pitch for 14 more seasons after the surgery. He finished his career with 288 wins, more than 2,200 strikeouts, and a 26-year MLB career that ties for the third-longest in league history.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2089050610020610528

The procedure that saved his career now bears his name. More than 2,600 big leaguers and thousands more amateurs have since had UCL reconstructions. Shohei Ohtani, Jacob deGrom, Stephen Strasburg, John Smoltz — all owe entire second acts to a surgery that didn't exist until one pitcher and one doctor decided to try.

In a message the Yankees shared on August 8, John wrote his own goodbye. "Thanks to Dr. Jobe, who saved my arm & made it possible for me to continue pitching," John said in his Aug. 8 post. "That surgery has since gone on to save the careers of countless" pitchers who followed him.

He was the first. Because of him, he wasn't the last.

Von Miller Goes Home to Dallas

Von Miller is a Cowboy. The DeSoto, Texas native who starred at Texas A&M is finally suiting up for the team he grew up watching.

https://twitter.com/AdamSchefter/status/2089156044098240543

https://twitter.com/AlbertBreer/status/2089175482767938015

Miller is 37 and has been on his own personal free agent farewell tour — Broncos, Rams, Bills, Commanders, and now Dallas in what will be his 15th NFL season. He's tied with former Broncos teammate DeMarcus Ware for ninth on the NFL's all-time sack leaders list with 138.5 sacks. He needs just 3.5 to pass Michael Strahan (141.5) for sixth.

The scouting report on Von at 37 comes from the man himself. In a social media from Aug. 13, the edge rusher said he could "roll out the bed and get 8 sacks." His track record backs it up — he put up 9 sacks last year in Washington while playing 37% of the snaps.
