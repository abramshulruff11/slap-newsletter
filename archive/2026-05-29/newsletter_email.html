Claude Lemieux, a four-time Stanley Cup champion and 1995 Conn Smythe Trophy winner, died by suicide Thursday at age 60.

Lemieux won the Stanley Cup with Montreal in 1986, New Jersey in 1995 and 2000, and Colorado in 1996. In 1995, he won the Conn Smythe Trophy as playoff MVP after leading all skaters with 13 goals for the New Jersey Devils. Lemieux was the Montreal Canadiens' torch bearer Monday night before Game 3 of the Eastern Conference Final at Bell Centre. He played 1,215 games in the NHL, scoring 786 points (379 goals, 407 assists). His 80 career playoff goals rank ninth in league history. The NHL Alumni Association said "He was loved by his wife and four children, and on behalf of the Lemieux family, we kindly ask that everyone respect their privacy during this difficult time."

NBA Passes Radical Anti-Tanking Lottery Reform

The NBA's Board of Governors passed new anti-tanking rules 29-1. The draft lottery expands from 14 to 16 teams, flattens odds even further, and creates a relegation zone that penalizes the bottom three teams. The 3-2-1 Lottery starts with the 2027 NBA Draft and runs through 2029.

Here's how it works:

- Bottom three teams get TWO lottery balls (the "relegation zone")
- Teams 4-10 who miss the playoffs get THREE balls each
- The 9 and 10 seeds in each conference get TWO balls
- Losers of the 7-8 play-in games get ONE ball
- All 16 picks are drawn randomly

A team cannot win back-to-back lotteries or pick inside the top five for three consecutive drafts. San Antonio landed the first, fourth and second picks in 2023, 2024 and 2025 — Victor Wembanyama, Stephon Castle, Dylan Harper. That ends now.

Memphis cast the only no vote. When the Grizzlies traded Jaren Jackson Jr. in February, one of the picks they received was the most favorable 2027 selection between Utah, Minnesota and Cleveland. The Jazz picked No. 5 in 2025 and No. 2 in 2026. That makes their pick ineligible for the top five in next year's draft. Memphis is getting punished for Utah's tanking.

The Washington Wizards lost 27 of their final 28 games to ensure the league's worst record at 17-65. Trae Young and Anthony Davis — acquired in December and February — played a combined five games.

The NBA just became the first American pro sports league to actively punish the worst teams in the draft.

MLB Proposes Hard Salary Cap for First Time Since 1994

MLB proposed a salary-cap system Thursday. Hard cap of $245.3 million. Hard floor of $171.2 million. The last time MLB proposed a salary cap was in 1994. The players went on strike and the World Series was canceled. The MLBPA proposed tens of millions in extra revenue sharing to go to low-revenue teams that make the postseason or have a winning record. The players do not want a cap. Teams currently spending more than $245 million: Mets, Dodgers, Yankees, Blue Jays, Phillies, Braves. Houston sneaks in at $240 million. Currently below the proposed salary floor: 50% of the league, starting with the Orioles at $167 million and ending with the Marlins at $78 million.

Two-thirds of the league would need to adjust their payrolls. The current CBA expires December 1.
