Mike Ditka Dies at 86

Mike Ditka, the Hall of Fame tight end and Super Bowl-winning head coach of the 1985 Chicago Bears, died Friday. He was 86.

George Halas drafted Ditka out of Pittsburgh in 1961, and after a falling out that led to Halas trading Ditka in the prime of his career in 1967, Halas hand-picked Ditka to coach the Bears in 1982. He won NFL Rookie of the Year that first season, made five straight Pro Bowls, and was a key part of the Bears' 1963 NFL championship team.

After leaving Chicago, he won Super Bowl VI as a player with the Cowboys and Super Bowl XII as a Dallas assistant under Tom Landry. Then came the call back home. In 1985, he led the Bears to Super Bowl XX, a 46-10 win over the Patriots that remains the only Super Bowl title in franchise history.

Ditka and Tom Flores are the only people to win an NFL title as a player, an assistant coach, and a head coach. Ditka, Flores and Gary Kubiak are also the only people in modern NFL history to win a championship as head coach of a team he played for previously. Ditka is the only person to participate in both of the last two Chicago Bears' championships, as a player in 1963 and as head coach in 1985.

In 11 seasons as head coach, Ditka compiled a 112-68 record while leading the Bears to six NFC Central titles, three NFC Championship Games and a Super Bowl XX victory. He was named NFL Coach of the Year in 1985 and 1988. He later coached the New Orleans Saints for three seasons.

Inducted into both the College Football and Pro Football Halls of Fame, Ditka was named to the NFL's 50th, 75th, and 100th Anniversary All-Time Teams. His No. 89 is retired by the Bears.

He is survived by his wife Diana and four children.
