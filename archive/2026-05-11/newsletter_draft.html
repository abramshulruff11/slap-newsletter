Washington won. No. 1 pick. First time since 2010 when they took John Wall.

The Clippers got the No. 5 pick via the Pacers. Indiana traded their pick with protections from 1-4 and 10-30 to get Ivica Zubac in February. The Pacers finished with the second-worst record in the NBA at 19-63. Their pick landed at No. 5. It went to the Clippers.

The Pacers had a 53.1 percent chance of keeping a top-four pick and a 47.9 percent chance of losing it at fifth or sixth. They bet on themselves. They lost.

Kevin Pritchard apologized on Twitter. The president of the franchise. For a trade he made in February. Zubac played five games for Indiana after the trade while dealing with injuries, including a fractured rib.

Five games. The pick is gone.

This is the last lottery under the current format. Since the odds changed in 2019, the worst team had never won the No. 1 overall pick. The Wizards, Jazz, Grizzlies and Bulls won the first four. In the final year of this format, that changes.

The system finally worked. Right before they replace it.

Back to the Pacers. 19-63 record. Best lottery odds. They valued Zubac because he could start at center — a position of need after Myles Turner left — and fit the budget. They thought they could get both. They got Zubac for five games and lost the pick.

The Clippers had a 47.9% chance of getting the pick from Indiana. Essentially a coin flip. They called heads. It came up heads. Indiana president Kevin Pritchard is now explaining to fans why they endured a 19-win season for Ivica Zubac.

Wemby Got His First Career Ejection for Elbowing Naz Reid in the Jaw

@SportsCenter
WEMBY EJECTED AFTER ELBOWING NAZ REID 😳 Victor Wembanyama received a flagrant-2 after elbowing Naz Reid in the second quarter of Game 4 vs. Minnesota.
View tweet

Wembanyama got his first career ejection Sunday in Game 4 of the Western Conference semifinals after throwing an elbow at the jaw of Naz Reid early in the second quarter.

The hit caught Reid on the right side of his jaw and neck area. Flagrant 2. Automatic ejection.

@HaterReport
Victor Wembanyama didn't realize he was ejected: Wemby: "What's that mean?" Harrison Barnes: "You're ejected" Wemby: "I'm ejected? Alright"
View tweet

Wembanyama asked teammate Harrison Barnes, "What does that mean?" when the penalty was announced. He didn't know what a flagrant-2 was. He finished with four points, four rebounds and three fouls in 13 minutes.

Wembanyama's ejection with 8:39 left in the second quarter is the earliest an All-Star has been tossed from a playoff game in the play-by-play era (since 1997-98). The Spurs lost 114-109. Series tied 2-2.

The Knicks Swept the Sixers So Bad That Embiid Doesn't Even Know If He'll Be Back Next Year

The Sixers season is over after being swept out of the playoffs by the New York Knicks in a 144-114 loss in Game 4 Sunday.

@SportsCenter
KNICKS SWEEP THE SIXERS 🧹 New York handles Philly in four-straight games to advance 💰
View tweet

Deuce McBride was 7-for-9 from three-point range Sunday. 25 points. Career playoff high. The Knicks took 29 first-half shots from three-point range and made 18 of them. 62%.

The Knicks were 11-of-13 from deep in the first quarter and 15-of-22 overall. They pulled out to a 43-24 lead in the first quarter and never looked back.

The Knicks have outscored their opponents by 19.4 points per game through two playoff rounds — the best per-game differential for any team through two rounds in the 43 years of the 16-team format. They have the second-ranked offense and third-ranked defense in these playoffs. They scored 129.1 points per 100 possessions in this series, 14.7 more than the Sixers allowed in the regular season — the biggest such differential in the 12 series of these playoffs.
