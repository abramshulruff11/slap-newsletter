The Knicks Parade Was a 2 Million Person Thing

New York threw its first championship parade in 53 years and compressed every viral moment it's ever produced into one Thursday morning.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2067787436705407433

Two million people lined Broadway. First ticker-tape parade in Knicks history — they didn't get one for 1970 or 1973. Mayor Mamdani handed out keys to the city. Alicia Keys closed with Empire State of Mind. Timothée Chalamet rode a float. Spike Lee cried. The basketball players were almost a footnote.

Tyler Kolek, member of the championship team, showed up and got mistaken for a fan trying to jump the barricade.

Most New York moment of the year:

Guy literally regains consciousness and his first instinct is to commit assault. Welcome back to Earth, king. That's a $400 deductible and a restraining order in one motion.

Carmelo Anthony showed up to remind everyone that the receipts have expiration dates.

https://twitter.com/TheNBACentel/status/2067644241614876964

Melo dragged this team to a 2nd round in 2013. Jalen Brunson — who they got for the price of a used Honda Civic — is the one getting MVP chants.

The parade was also a soft launch for The Josh Hart Marriage Industrial Complex. Hart's wife reportedly stuck her thumb up his rear end on the float, which we are told is payback for Brunson's playoff oil check earlier this spring. Six months of marital warfare leveraged into a parade prank. That is championship-level pettiness and I have to respect it.

Wemby was apparently heckled by Knicks fans during the parade because of course he was. The man lost the Finals five days ago and New Yorkers are already heckling him on Broadway. There is no recovery period. There is no grace period. There is only the city.

For scale: NYPD reported 13 arrests. Kolek was almost the 14th.
