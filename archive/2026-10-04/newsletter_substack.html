Brewers Survive Padres 3-2 in NLDS Opener After the Roof Robbed Ty France

Milwaukee and San Diego played a two-and-a-half-hour knife fight and the deciding blow came from a rookie fastball and a seventh-inning swing. William Contreras' go-ahead solo shot in the seventh inning proved decisive in a 3-2 win at American Family Field, and before any of that, Jacob Misiorowski set the tone by throwing five pitches at 104+ mph in the first inning alone. The kid is 23 years old and throws harder than most team's bullpens combined. Then the ninth happened. One out, runner on first, Padres down one. Ty France turns on a 1-0 Trevor Megill fastball and sends it toward the left field roof. The ball hit a cable on the American Family Field roof, and Jackson Chourio caught it at the left field warning track. Under the ballpark's ground rules, a ball that hits the roof, a truss or a cable over fair territory is in play and can be caught for an out.

Ty France watching the ball he just crushed get swallowed by a roof cable:

The scenario came up during the lineup exchange before the game. Brewers third base coach Matt Erickson credited umpire Nic Lentz for going over that specific ground rule. "He gets through the whole thing and I looked at everybody and said, 'I haven't seen it in a while, but it's definitely happening tonight.'" The umpires rehearsed this rule four hours before it decided a playoff game.

Padres fans learning that the umpires held a pre-game seminar on the exact rule that would end their night:

France hit the ball 105.3 mph at a 49-degree launch angle. Even after hitting the roof, it traveled 325 feet. Statcast says it would've been a home run in one MLB ballpark — Daikin Park in Houston. It's an out in 29 parks regardless. The roof is only the villain if you squint. France still thinks he got robbed, Contreras told reporters he thought it was gone, and Padres manager Craig Stammen called it a bad break.

Rays One-Hit the Yankees 1-0 — Rasmussen Carries a No-No Into the 8th, Wells Blows Through a Stop Sign

Drew Rasmussen made the Yankees look like a Low-A roster. Rasmussen carried a no-hitter into the eighth inning and put together a complete masterpiece on the mound in Game 1 of the American League Division Series, blanking the Yankees in the Rays' 1-0 win before a rowdy, sold-out home crowd. Rasmussen is the seventh pitcher in the Modern Era to carry a no-hit bid through 7 2/3 innings in the postseason and the first since Aníbal Sánchez did the same for the Nationals against the Cardinals in Game 1 of the 2019 National League Championship Series. He racked up a career-high 23 swinging strikes, the most by a Ray in a postseason game, mixing cutters, changeups, four-seamers and two-seamers until the Bronx bats surrendered.
