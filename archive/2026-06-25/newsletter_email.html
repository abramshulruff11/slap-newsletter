Lions CB Terrion Arnold Arrested, Facing Life in Prison

Detroit Lions cornerback Terrion Arnold turned himself in at Orient Road Jail in Hillsborough County on Wednesday night. Four counts of kidnapping. Four counts of armed robbery. Maximum sentence: life in prison.

@AdamSchefter
Lions CB Terrion Arnold turned himself in and was taken into custody at Orient Road Jail in Hillsborough County on Wednesday night. He faces four counts of kidnapping and four counts of armed robbery, which carry a potential sentence of up to life in prison.
View on Twitter →

Eight felony counts tied to a violent incident at a Tampa apartment in February.

The story: Tampa police spent four months investigating Arnold's involvement in a Feb. 4 kidnapping and robbery at a Largo, Florida, Airbnb rented by Arnold and several friends. Someone broke in while Arnold's group was out. Items stolen: $100,000 in cash and an $80,000 necklace. Arnold called the police and reported more than $250,000 in property losses.

What happened next is what landed him in jail. Arnold allegedly orchestrated a kidnapping and robbery to recover the stolen property: watches, jewelry, designer bags and shoes, a cell phone, and an estimated $100,000 in cash.

@AdamSchefter
Lions cornerback Terrion Arnold is facing multiple felony charges after he was arrested Wednesday in Tampa in connection with a robbery and kidnapping in early February. Story via @E_Woodyard: https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/49172395/lions-terrion-arnold-arrested-faces-felony-charges-kidnapping-armed-robbery
View on Twitter →

The group allegedly lured a driver and two other men to an apartment where two of the victims were jumped, pistol-whipped and held at gunpoint by two of Arnold's associates, Lyndell Hudson II and Christion Williams. A third man made his way into the apartment. He was also jumped. All three had visible injuries. Arnold wasn't in the apartment but watched via live stream set up by one of his associates, according to police. Arnold's crew let the three men go but stole their personal property.

Tampa police later confirmed the three teenage victims were completely innocent. No involvement in the original theft.

Arnold maintains his innocence. The statement from his camp via Tom Pelissero:

@TomPelissero
Terrion Arnold voluntarily surrendered himself tonight to authorities. Statement on his behalf from @DeniseWhite_EAG:
View on Twitter →

His attorneys: the government is relying on testimony from convicted felons who have admitted involvement and may have substantial incentives to shift blame.

The Lions released a statement. "We're aware." Dan Campbell had previously defended Arnold. "As far as I know it's not a big deal. It seems like he still wasn't involved with this." Team president Rod Wood told the Detroit Free Press that Arnold "was in my office the day after the allegations came out" and was "grilled" about what happened.

That posture aged poorly. The State Attorney's Office filed a pretrial detention motion to keep Arnold jailed until trial. Their argument: he remains a danger to the community.
