Carolina Is One Win From Ending a 20-Year Cup Drought, and Rod Brind'Amour Is Living the Hockey Movie

Carolina won Game 5 at home 4-2. The Hurricanes lead the Stanley Cup Final 3-2. One more win and they get their first Cup since 2006 — the only one in franchise history, the one Rod Brind'Amour captained.

That last sentence is the whole story.

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2065266837319798802

Brind'Amour captained the Hurricanes to the Cup in 2006. Twenty years later he's behind the bench, four wins into the Final, one win from doing it again in a different chair.

The "win as player, win as coach with the same franchise" club is the most exclusive room in pro sports. In the NBA you've got Bill Russell, Pat Riley, Billy Cunningham, Tom Heinsohn, K.C. Jones, Tyronn Lue. Red Holzman won a championship as a player and later coached the Knicks to two championships in 1970 and 1973 — yes, the same 1973 Knicks title these current Knicks are trying to match. Everything in sports is connected this week.

The point is the list of people who've done what Brind'Amour is trying to do is short, sacred, and full of names you've heard your whole life. Sunday he can add his.

And he didn't get here on vibes. Jordan Staal is busy chiseling his own statue.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2065234203139088755

Six goals in five Cup Final games. Staal is the current captain, wearing the C that Brind'Amour wore in 2006, scoring at will against a team that paid $88 million in free agency to never let this happen. Vegas hates it.

Then there's the goalie situation.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2065280881858695232

Read that again. His first two career playoff starts. In the Stanley Cup Final. Won both. The guy didn't have a playoff résumé six days ago and now he's the first goalie ever to do this. The NHL has been around since 1917 and BusBus is rewriting the record book on a learner's permit.

Vegas is not coping well with the idea of losing to a backup goalie making his second career playoff start. John Tortorella is yelling about it.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2065285921637880317

Torts saying there will be a Game 7 is a man speaking it into existence with his eyes closed. Vegas has to win in Raleigh-adjacent road conditions Sunday, then come home and win another. Possible. Not likely. Not against this defense and not against a city that has waited two decades for this exact phone call.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2065266316563317086

Sunday in Vegas. If Carolina wins, Brind'Amour hoists the Cup as a coach where he hoisted it as a captain. If they don't, we get a Game 7 at home Tuesday with the weight of the entire franchise's history pressing on one sheet of ice.

The Knicks Comeback Is Still the Only Thing Anyone Wants to Talk About

Three days later. Three. Days. And the entire internet is still picking apart the largest comeback in NBA Finals history like it's the Zapruder film.

The Knicks' 29-point comeback was the largest in Finals history, surpassing Game 4 of the 2008 series between the Boston Celtics and the Los Angeles Lakers, where Boston erased a 24-point deficit. At one point in the fourth quarter, San Antonio's win probability was 99.6%, per ESPN Analytics, meaning the Knicks had a 1-in-250 chance at a comeback.

It happened. The 1-in-250 happened. And it happened because De'Aaron Fox decided to shoot a layup with 12 seconds left and a three-point lead instead of dribbling out the clock.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2065194528928157924

Correct No Call. Translation: nope, Fox just made a bad decision and got hunted for it. Clean. The league spent 24 hours studying the tape so they could deliver that verdict in the most surgical way possible.

Mike Brown — current Knicks head coach, the guy who drew up the comeback — was FIRED in 2024 from Sacramento after publicly calling out Fox for doing this EXACT thing.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2065281079766814850

Mike Brown got fired for being right about De'Aaron Fox. Two years later, Mike Brown is on the other bench when De'Aaron Fox does the exact thing Mike Brown got fired for warning him about. And Mike Brown wins the Finals because of it.

And then there's KAT — who was apparently coaching from the bench.
