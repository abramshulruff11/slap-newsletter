The Sixers Finally Did It: 3-1 Comeback Over Boston Ends 44-Year Curse

Philadelphia 76ers 109, Boston Celtics 100. The Sixers came back from 3-1 down to win a playoff series for the first time in franchise history, and beat Boston in a playoff series for the first time since 1982.

https://twitter.com/TheNBACentel/status/2050758802794762693

The Celtics had never blown a 3-1 lead. Ever. In franchise history. 32 previous times they took a 3-1 lead, 32 times they closed it out. Until Joel Embiid showed up.

https://twitter.com/TimBontemps/status/2050758120561910092

Embiid led Philadelphia with 34 points, 12 rebounds and six assists, while Maxey added 30, 11 and seven. Embiid had never before won a Game 7 in three previous tries. Toronto heartbreak. Atlanta upset. Boston blowout. All losses. Until tonight.

Jayson Tatum was ruled out for Game 7 with left knee stiffness. The team announced the news about 90 minutes before the start of the winner-take-all first-round matchup. Without Tatum, Boston went with regulars Jaylen Brown and Derrick White to go along with Baylor Scheierman, Ron Harper Jr. and Luka Garza. Three starters shot a combined 0-for-7 from the floor. The Celtics became the first team ever to have three starters in a playoff game finish with zero points — Luka Garza, Baylor Scheierman, and Ron Harper Jr. First time since 1970.
The Sixers had won just three playoff games in Boston in a 44-year span since 1983. They were 3-17 in 20 road playoff games in Boston since the 1982 Eastern Conference Finals. Then they won three straight there in a week.

Padres Sold for Record $3.9 Billion: New Owner 'Eye on World Series'

The San Diego Padres have been sold to José E. Feliciano, Clearlake Capital co-founder and part of the Chelsea FC ownership group, and his wife Kwanza Jones. The sale price is an MLB-record $3.9 billion.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2050795041631986052

The price shatters the record for an MLB franchise sale, previously set when Steve Cohen bought the New York Mets for $2.42 billion in 2020. The Padres remain one of five MLB franchises that have never won a World Series. New owner immediately says "eye on World Series" — bold for a team that's never done it. Peter Seidler joined the Padres' ownership group in 2012 when John Moores sold the team for $800 million. Seidler took over and immediately endeared himself to San Diego's fans with his aggressive financial backing of general manager A.J. Preller, who built a team that has reached the playoffs in four of the past six years.
