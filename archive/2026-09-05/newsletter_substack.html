Baby Jesus Arrives: Malachi Toney, 18, Breaks 42-Year-Old Miami Record with 229 Yards and Three TDs in Less Than Three Quarters

Malachi Toney is 18 years old. He plays wide receiver for the Miami Hurricanes. Last night against Stanford, he caught passes for 229 yards and three touchdowns, and he was done before the fourth quarter started.

https://twitter.com/SharpFootball/status/2096079372931281105

The record he broke belonged to Eddie Brown. Brown was a consensus first-team All-American, went 13th overall to Cincinnati, and won Offensive Rookie of the Year. That mark stood at The U — the actual U, the WR factory, the school that produced Michael Irvin and Reggie Wayne and Andre Johnson and Santana Moss — for decades. A high school senior from a year ago just broke it in three quarters.

The kid also came with a personality package.

https://twitter.com/Ihartitz/status/2096043949823537572

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2096079212797198454

Pat McAfee is calling him Baby Jesus. Barstool Big Cat has declared everyone who doubted him is in hell. The kid himself is out here posing for a trophy that gets handed out in December, in September, in Week 1, after his first career touchdown.

Stanford is not good. Miami hung 45 on them and Darian Mensah's first half was 18-of-21 for 253 yards and three scores — the whole passing game was on skates.

But 229 yards as a true freshman in his first meaningful college game is 229 yards.

Miami plays actual defenses this year. The "Heisman dark horse in September" pipeline is a well-worn road to being forgotten by Halloween. But if Toney is still hitting the pose in November against Florida State, we're going to be talking about him for years.

Guardians Sweep Doubleheader on Two Walk-Offs, and Brayan Rocchio Did Something Absurd

Cleveland played two games against Detroit yesterday and walked off in both of them.

Game 1: Brayan Rocchio, a switch-hitter, came up in a big spot batting lefty and hit the game-tying grand slam. His first career grand slam. Then, later in the same game, he came up batting righty and hit the walk-off solo homer. 405 feet, career-longest. Different sides of the plate. Same game. Both game-changers. Final: 7-6.

Game 2 was Travis Bazzana's turn. Home run in the 8th to tie it, walk-off single to win it. 4-3.

Cleveland took the doubleheader on two walk-offs in the same day, delivered by two players you couldn't pick out of a lineup at a Guardians autograph signing. They don't have Judge or Ohtani. They have Rocchio switching sides of the plate to break your heart twice.

Campusano Walks Off Yankees, Ben Rice's 36th HR Not Enough

Ben Rice hit his 36th home run of the season for the Yankees. Luis Campusano hit a two-run walk-off homer for the Padres. Padres 3, Yankees 2.

Ben Rice is a rookie catcher/first baseman and it's not even the story of the game he played in. Welcome to the Yankees, where the best individual performance of your season still ends with you watching the other dugout dogpile at home plate.

Serena and Venus Bow Out of US Open Doubles, Arthur Ashe Rises

Serena and Venus Williams played their first US Open doubles match in years last night. They lost in the first round in three sets. The crowd at Arthur Ashe stood up and stayed standing as the sisters walked off together.

Together, they won 14 Grand Slam women's doubles titles while compiling a perfect 14-0 record in Grand Slam doubles finals and captured three Olympic gold medals. Fourteen and zero in Slam finals. Three Olympic golds. A career doubles Grand Slam. All of it done as a side project while they were also two of the best singles players of all time.

The result didn't matter. Everyone in the building knew what they were watching.
