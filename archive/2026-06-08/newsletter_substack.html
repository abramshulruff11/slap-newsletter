Stacey King, voice of the Bulls, dies at 59

Stacey King passed away Sunday at 59. The Bulls announced it. No cause of death has been released.

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2063670259387609444

https://twitter.com/ChrisBHaynes/status/2063672052368777484

King was the sixth overall pick out of Oklahoma in 1989. He won three championships with the Bulls from 1991 to 1993. Then Minnesota, Miami, Dallas, Boston. Playing career over by 1997.

For an entire generation of Bulls fans, King was the voice in their living room. Color commentator from 2006-07 through 2025-26. Twenty straight years calling games next to Neil Funk, then Adam Amin, through Derrick Rose's MVP run, through the tank years, through everything.

https://twitter.com/ESPN/status/2063677776901620118

He gave Chicago the catchphrases that became the soundtrack of two decades of Bulls basketball. Emmy Award-winning broadcaster. Famous for his nicknames and signature calls — "Gimme the hot sauce," "Let me step back and kiss myself," and "Drive home safely, Chicago! Beep, beep!" He nicknamed Jimmy Butler "Jimmy G. Buckets." He called Derrick Rose the "Windy City assassin."

https://twitter.com/W_B_Rick/status/2063674861906473427

If you grew up watching the Bulls in the 2010s, you heard that call live. You can probably still do the impression.

https://twitter.com/W_B_Rick/status/2063672090729869803

https://twitter.com/BarstoolBigCat/status/2063671333653758248

King didn't have to be good at broadcasting. Plenty of ex-players collect a check and phone it in. King made it his life's work. No matter how bad the Bulls got, Stacey made them worth watching. Through the post-Rose rebuild, through the Lauri Markkanen years, through the LaVine-DeRozan-Vucevic plateau — he was the reason a lot of people kept the TV on.

He won three rings with Michael Jordan. He spent the next thirty years giving Chicago something just as valuable: a reason to laugh on a Tuesday night in February when the Bulls were down 18 to the Hornets.

Drive home safely, Chicago.

Marner Just Broke a 69-Year-Old Rocket Richard Record

Stanley Cup Final Game 4 is tonight in Vegas. Knights lead 2-1. They lead because Mitch Marner just had one of the most absurd periods in the history of the sport.

Marner scored the fastest hat trick in Cup Final history in the second period of Vegas's 5-4 double overtime win Saturday. Three goals in 6:10. Previous record: 6:21, Maurice "Rocket" Richard, 69 years earlier.

https://twitter.com/PFTCommenter/status/2063692735509647398

Rocket Richard. The guy they named the goal-scoring trophy after. The guy in the Hall of Fame. Marner just took his record by 11 seconds. He's also only the seventh player in NHL history to score a natural hat trick in the Cup Final. Gordie Howe, Ted Lindsay, Newsy Lalonde from 1919.

The same Mitch Marner who spent his entire Toronto career being treated as the avatar of playoff softness. Sign-and-traded to Vegas last July. Now leading the playoffs in scoring with 28 points and front-running the Conn Smythe.

The cruelest part for Carolina: the Hurricanes roared back from down 4-0 in Game 3 with four third-period goals, three coming in 39 seconds, before Vegas won 5-4 in double overtime. They blew a 4-0 lead in a Cup Final game, came all the way back to tie it, and STILL lost on Shea Theodore in double OT.

Game 4 is tonight. Knights can take a 3-1 stranglehold.

Christian Eriksen Collapsed Again. His Pacemaker Did Its Job.

Christian Eriksen clutched his chest and collapsed in the 65th minute of a Denmark-Ukraine friendly on Sunday. The ICD — the implantable defibrillator fitted after his cardiac arrest at Euro 2020 — activated exactly as designed. The Danish team doctor confirmed the device worked flawlessly. Eriksen was conscious, stable, and transported to the hospital. The match was abandoned.

Five years after he died on the pitch in Copenhagen and was brought back, the technology in his chest did its job again. There's no joke here. Just gratitude that the device exists, that the medical staff was ready, and that he's still here.
