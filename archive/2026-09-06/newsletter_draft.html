Michigan Gets the Call

Michigan was a 27.5-point favorite at home against a MAC team. With seconds left, Bryce Underwood heaved a Hail Mary that landed in the arms of a Western Michigan defender out of bounds. Clock: 0:00. Western Michigan players started celebrating on the field. Then the refs went to review.

One second went back on the clock.

@HaterReport
RIGGED

Ball in the air and the clock is very CLEARLY at ZERO! Yet the referees added 1 second for Michigan leading to a walk-off Hail Mary win.

INSANE
View tweet

NBC officiating analyst Terry McAulay suggested that the stadium clock used by officials during the replay review may have been different than the clock seen on the broadcast replay. Two different clocks disagreed and the refs picked the one Michigan needed.

Second chance. Same play call. Underwood rolled left, backpedaled, and this time found JJ Buchanan in the end zone for a 47-yard touchdown. 13-12. Michigan wins a game that ended twice.

Michigan fans stormed the field. Against Western Michigan. In a game they were favored to win by four touchdowns. After the refs had to intervene to make it possible.

Even Michigan's own locker room read the room. "Sick to my stomach is the way I explain my feelings about that game," Michigan defensive end Dom Nichols said. That's the winner talking.

The Big Ten released a statement two hours later with a video from the in-stadium clock defending the call. The league itself spent a Saturday night publishing evidence that its own refs didn't rob a MAC team on national television.

Louisiana Lane Has Arrived

Lane Kiffin's LSU era began with a pick-six roughly 30 seconds into the game. Clemson quarterback Christopher Vizzina threw it. Dabo Swinney's rehired offensive coordinator Chad Morris designed the drive. It never got better from there.

@ESPN
LOUISIANA LANE HAS ARRIVED IN BATON ROUGE 🐯

LSU DOMINATES CLEMSON TO BEGIN ITS SEASON 🔥
View tweet

Final: LSU 51, Clemson 10. Up 41, Kiffin went for two.

Kiffin's postgame line — "imagine if we had pro players" — was a shot at the offseason ruling that keeps NFL guys out of college football. It was also a shot at Dabo, who has spent a decade lecturing everyone about doing it the "right way" and just got waxed by 41 in his own conference-adjacent opener. Dabo has now hit the annual "Dabo is done" news cycle by the first Saturday in September, which might be a personal record.
