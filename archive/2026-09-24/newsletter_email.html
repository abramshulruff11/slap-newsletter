Four postseason misses. Three first-round exits. One pennant. Zero rings.

Now the winter. Vlad is a free agent. He can re-sign with the only organization he's ever known — the one that just posted an eight-year highlight reel of getting knocked out — or he can walk. Ken Griffey Jr. spent a decade in Seattle before he got out. Barry Bonds left Pittsburgh. Ernie Banks never left, and never played a playoff game.

The Worst Division Race in MLB History Is Happening Right Now

Josh Naylor walked off the Astros last night to tie the AL West at 78-80. Both teams. Same record. Four games left. Winner hosts a playoff game. Loser goes home.

The worst division winner in MLB history is the 2005 San Diego Padres, who went 82-80. Whoever wins this thing is going to be immortalized as the answer to a trivia question nobody wants their franchise attached to.

Yainer Diaz put Houston ahead with a go-ahead triple in the 10th. Naylor ended it in the bottom half. Two teams playing October baseball for the right to finish under .500.

Lonzo Ball Retires at 28

Lonzo Ball is done. Twenty-eight years old. The knee never came all the way back.

@NBAMemes
Lonzo Ball just announced his retirement from the NBA at only 28 years old due to his career-altering knee injury.
💔💔
View on Twitter →

Second overall pick by the Lakers in 2017. Traded to New Orleans in the Anthony Davis deal, where he had the two best years of his career. Signed a four-year, $80 million deal with Chicago in 2021 and was playing the best basketball of his life through 40 games — the Bulls were 27-13 — when he tore his meniscus in January 2022. He would miss over 1,000 days. Three surgeries on the left knee, including a cartilage transplant in 2023.

He made it back to the floor with Chicago in 2024-25 and finished last season in Cleveland. The passing, the transition vision, the length on defense — you could see all of it before the knee. You just never got to see what it looked like at 26.

Twenty-eight is when guys are supposed to be entering their prime. Brandon Roy. Penny Hardaway. Grant Hill before Phoenix. The list of guards who lost the back half of their careers to their own bodies is short, and Lonzo is now on it.
