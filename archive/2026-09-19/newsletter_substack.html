Aaron Judge, Back for a Week

Aaron Judge returned from a three-month absence on September 8. He played seven games. Now he's back on the IL.

https://twitter.com/JeffPassan/status/2101060213914612004

Seven games. 4-for-23. One homer. Thirteen strikeouts.

The Yankees are in the playoff hunt. Five games back with ten to play. 5.5 games up on Boston. What's actually at stake now is whether this team can beat Boston in a wild card series without their captain.

The calf is the part that should scare you. Ribs heal on a timeline. Calves keep coming back the moment you plant wrong — ask Kevin Durant, who pushed through one and left on a stretcher. When Judge was asked what he was dealing with, he said "lower body." The Yankees called it "leg tightness." When the team stops giving specifics, the specifics are usually bad.

66 games. Judge has played 66 games this season. Not counting his cup of coffee in 2016 or COVID-shortened 2020, he's never played fewer.

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2101067918406782993

Assuming the roster move is backdated to Thursday, he won't be eligible to return until Sept. 27 — the Yankees' final game of the regular season. Best case, he gets one tune-up at-bat before the wild card. Worst case, he's already done.

Kelsey Mitchell, Record Breaker

Kelsey Mitchell scored 33 in Toronto on Friday and broke A'ja Wilson's single-season WNBA scoring record. She found out about it after the game, when she walked into the locker room and saw confetti.

Her first thought was that coach Stephanie White had passed a milestone. Then everybody started coming her way.

1,034 points on the season. Wilson's previous record was 1,021, set in 2024. And Mitchell isn't the only one in Indiana operating in a different reality right now.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2101125384578199954

Clark has scored 25+ and dished 10+ assists ten times this season. One teammate breaks the all-time scoring record, the other is on a career no one else has ever had. In one season.

The Fever beat Toronto 103-85 on Friday night. Eighth win in ten games. Nobody wants to see this team in October.

Braves Back, Olson at 40

The Braves are going to the playoffs. Again.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2101142557245821253

A year ago Atlanta finished under .500 and got left for dead. The window shut, Acuña finished, the rotation collapsed. Turns out you don't rebuild a team — you just wait for it to be healthy again.

Everyone who spent 2025 writing the Braves obituary:

Matt Olson hit his 40th home run on Friday. Second time in his career — the first was 2023, when he led the league with 54. He does this quietly, in Atlanta, while the internet argues about Judge and Ohtani. Every year you look up in September and he's got 40 bombs and 100 RBI.

Then Acuña climbed the wall to rob Altuve of extra bases with the Astros creeping back into it. The offense clinched the game. The defense sealed it. October is going to be a problem for somebody.

Darian Mensah, 24 Straight

Miami's Darian Mensah completed his first 24 passes against Wake Forest. Not 24 in a row across a couple of drives — the first 24 he threw all game.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2101116546521432216

Mensah did it in the first two quarters of a September road game.

https://twitter.com/FieldYates/status/2101116838264582348

61 of 65. Through ten quarters. That's a completion percentage that shouldn't exist outside of a video game on rookie sliders. Miami won 33-20 and The U being back is starting to sound less like a bit.
