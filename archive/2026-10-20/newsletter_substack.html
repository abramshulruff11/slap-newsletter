Rays Beat Guardians, Head to World Series vs Brewers — First-Time Champion Guaranteed

Tampa Bay 7, Cleveland 3. Series over. The Rays are going to the Fall Classic to face Milwaukee — and one of these two franchises will lift a Commissioner's Trophy for the first time in their history. That has happened exactly once in the last 100 years.

Baseball fans in Tampa and Milwaukee right now:

Freddy Peralta is your ALCS MVP. He went 5-9 with a 4.99 ERA in 22 starts for the Mets, got traded Aug. 2, then went 5-2 with a 3.25 ERA in 10 starts for Tampa Bay. Named AL pitcher of the month for September. Then he pitched in three ALCS games and put up a 0.75 ERA across 12 innings — Tampa won every one of them.

Peralta now faces Milwaukee, the team that gave him his big-league debut and traded him last winter, in the World Series. The Brewers watching their former ace carry a different team to a title shot against them:

Tampa's first World Series since 2020. Third pennant in franchise history. The Rays, who have never cleared a $120M payroll, pulled this off by picking up a struggling Mets pitcher at the deadline and letting their pitching coach cook.

Cleveland: last title 1948. Harry Truman was president. The franchise's last World Series trip was 2016, when they got Zobrist'd by the Cubs. Now they go home with a core — José Ramírez, Steven Kwan, the whole lot — facing another winter of "when, exactly."

49ers Stay Perfect at 6-0, Jayden Daniels Throws It Away on 4th Down — McLaurin Carted Off

San Francisco 23, Washington 17. The Niners are 6-0. The Commanders are 1-4-1. But the moment of the game had nothing to do with the score.

Second quarter. 4th down. Jayden Daniels drops back, feels pressure, and throws the ball away. On fourth down. Turnover on downs. Dan Quinn on the sideline looked like a man who also did not know it was fourth down.

Second straight week Daniels made this exact flavor of mental error. Last week he spiked the ball to stop the clock with zero timeouts remaining. This week he threw the ball away on fourth down. The reigning Offensive Rookie of the Year is operating with the game-management IQ of a guy who just unpaused Madden.

Angel Reese Demands the Ball, Drops 30-16 — Dream Even Finals 1-1

Angel Reese told anyone who would listen after Game 1 that she needed the ball. Monday night at Chase Center, the Dream gave it to her.

30 points. 16 rebounds. 3 assists. 2 blocks. Third player to score 30 points and grab 15 rebounds in a Finals game, joining Breanna Stewart (2020) and Jonquel Jones (2019). Youngest player in WNBA history to put up those numbers in a Finals game.

The win gave Atlanta its first victory in WNBA Finals history after 10 straight losses across three previous appearances. Dream fans had never seen a Finals W. Reese handed them one.

She was snarling at the crowd and letting everyone in Ballhalla know a defender who could contain her wasn't in the building. Series tied 1-1. Game 3 Wednesday in Atlanta.
