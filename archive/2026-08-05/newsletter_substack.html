The Lombard Brothers Both Homered in Their Debuts on the Same Night

George Lombard Jr. — Yankees top prospect, 21 years old, called up Tuesday after New York whiffed at the trade deadline and demoted Anthony Volpe — made his MLB debut against the Cardinals.

His younger brother Jacob — Marlins first-round pick, 18 years old, 14th overall in the 2026 draft — made his professional debut for Single-A Jupiter about 1,000 miles south.

Both hit home runs. In their second at-bats. Off hanging breaking balls. To left-center.

https://twitter.com/MLB/status/2084795275621675096

Exit velocities 0.3 mph apart (100.2 mph for George, 99.9 mph for Jacob). Same launch angle (33 degrees). Same field. Second plate appearance. If you wrote this into a script it would get sent back with "too on the nose."

The timing was the best part. Jacob went deep first. George Sr. watched Jacob's homer on his phone from the stands in Yankee Stadium. Jacob saw his brother's big fly during the game. "I peeked over at the TV and saw when he hit the bomb," Jacob said. "So I was pretty pumped for him."

George Jr. described his version:

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2084849201595167145

"We called a mound visit right as my brother was coming up to the plate. There was a TV in the bar, the last TV on the third base line, so I got to watch his AB and his home run. The TV shows my parents hugging and I was like 'Oh my, he just did that.'"

Dad is George Lombard Sr., six-year MLB veteran, current Tigers bench coach, 1994 second-round pick of the Braves. He's watching from Yankee Stadium — where his oldest son just went deep — with his phone in his hand as his youngest son also goes deep 1,000 miles away.

Mom didn't have words. "Just the fact that he's worked his whole life for this and that day had come, there was no words. For the first time in my life, I think, I didn't have words. I was just in shock."

George Jr.'s night wasn't just the homer. Backhanded stop and off-balanced throw to initiate a double play to end the second inning. Moments later, the home run off St. Louis starter Hunter Dobbins that doubled New York's lead.

Fourth-youngest Yankees infielder to homer in a game. Third-youngest Yankee to homer in his debut.

The Yankees needed this. They stayed quiet at the deadline. They optioned Volpe. They just handed the shortstop job to a 21-year-old whose dad is on a rival team's coaching staff, and he responded by turning two and hitting a bomb in his first game.
