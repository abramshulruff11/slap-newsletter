Ben Shelton closes with an ace at 3:33 a.m.

Ben Shelton just knocked out Carlos Alcaraz in a five-set epic at the US Open that ended at 3:33 a.m. ET — the latest finish in tournament history. After a 146 mph ace from Shelton to hold, Alcaraz blinked with a shot beyond the baseline, and Shelton blasted another 146 mph serve at 3:33 a.m. ET for another ace to seal the biggest win of his career.

The match started just after 11 p.m. ET and went four hours, 28 minutes. Shelton, the No. 8 seed, ended Alcaraz's long winning streak in Grand Slam tournaments. At one point the Spaniard could be seen vomiting into a towel.

Shelton moves on to face fellow American and No. 11 seed Frances Tiafoe in the semis, guaranteeing an American man will have a chance on Sunday to snap the drought since Andy Roddick lifted a grand slam title. The Shelton–Tiafoe match will be the first major meeting in the semifinals or later between Black American men in the Open era.

Big Foe has no quit

Frances Tiafoe was down two sets to Alex Michelsen. He kept coming back until he had reached his third US Open semifinal, beating Michelsen 5-7, 3-6, 7-5, 6-3, 7-6 (10-6). The biggest comeback of his career, on the biggest stage he plays on.

Then Tiafoe immediately went from celebrating his own win to consoling his friend at the net. Michelsen is 21, this was his breakthrough tournament, and Tiafoe knows exactly how it feels to be on the other side of that handshake.

The straw that stirs the drink is back

Aaron Judge played his first game since May 31. Six innings, as planned. Yankees beat the Rockies 5-3. He didn't need to hit a 500-foot bomb or make a diving catch. He just needed to be in the lineup, and the lineup won.

The Yankees clinched their 34th consecutive winning season. That is the second-longest streak in MLB history. The longest is 39, held by the Yankees, from 1926 to 1964.

The only franchise capable of beating the Yankees at being the Yankees is the Yankees.
