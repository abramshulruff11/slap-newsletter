Ben Shelton's Dream Run

Ben Shelton is going to the US Open final. First Black American man to reach a US Open final since Arthur Ashe in 1972.

https://twitter.com/ESPN/status/2098607523720798574

Fifty-four years. Ten presidents. The entire careers of Sampras, Agassi, Federer, Nadal, and Djokovic. Every single US Open ever played inside the stadium named after Arthur Ashe.

Tiafoe took the first set. Shelton adjusted, ripped off three straight, and casually threw a 158 MPH serve into the record books.

Shelton is serving a tennis ball 50% harder than Aroldis Chapman throws a baseball. After three sets.

Two friends. Two rising stars. One walks out to play for a Grand Slam trophy on Sunday.

Shelton spent half the match distracted by his own celebrity section. Trinity Rodman in the box. Jalen Brunson courtside. Matthew McConaughey and Woody Harrelson doing their own private broadcast in the stands.

Shelton said he looked up mid-match, saw Brunson on the jumbotron, and momentarily forgot he was playing a US Open semifinal. The most 22-year-old thing that has ever happened at Arthur Ashe Stadium.

Sunday brings Alexander Zverev — world No. 3, one match away from his second Slam in a single calendar year.

Shelton is playing for his first Slam and a piece of American tennis history that's been sitting untouched for 54 years. Zverev already said he hopes he "cannot make the American Dream come true on Sunday."

Junior Caminero's 40th Homer Sends Tampa to October

Junior Caminero came to the plate in the ninth with the Rays needing one swing to secure a postseason berth. Number 40 on the year, walk-off, ballgame over, playoffs locked.

Then he grabbed the mic and pulled out the LeBron.

"TAMPA, THIS IS FOR YOU."

Direct callback. Same cadence. Same energy. Except LeBron delivered his after winning a title and Caminero delivered his after clinching a Wild Card spot in September.

Drew Rasmussen threw seven innings and gave up one earned run. The Rays have now won his last ten starts. In a season where Tampa's rotation has been held together with tape and prayer, Rasmussen is the one guy who shows up and the team just wins.

Yandy Diaz celebrated by walking around the clubhouse eating a tomahawk steak.

Braves Refuse to Lose in 11 Innings

The Braves were down to their last out. Ha-seong Kim tied it. The Phillies took the lead back in the 11th on a Luiz Arraez knock. Then Drake Baldwin walked it off.

Six-to-five, Atlanta. Mike Yastrzemski had three hits including the ninth-inning tie. Sean Murphy had a game-tying knock earlier. Ronald Acuña Jr. was chasing foul balls into the stands like he owned the section.

Braves fans then got to skip straight from panic to euphoria.

Mason Miller Ends It at 103 MPH

The Athletics beat the Mariners 6-5 on a Donovan Walton walk-off two-run homer. Seattle had tied it in the ninth on a Lazaro Montes shot. Then Mason Miller came in and slammed the door with a 103 MPH strikeout painted on the corner.

103 MPH used to require a radar gun malfunction. Miller does it for the last out of a walk-off game like it's a warmup toss.
