Zolani Tete Dies at 38

Former two-division world champion boxer Zolani Tete was shot and killed outside his home in South Africa on Friday. He was 38.

Police said the shooting took place at Mdantsane, Eastern Cape, and that Tete was arriving at his residence and waiting for the gate to open when he was ambushed by two armed suspects wearing balaclavas. A 27-year-old woman who was in the vehicle with him was shot multiple times and is hospitalized.

Tete, known as "Last Born," grew up in Mdantsane, the Eastern Cape township that has produced generations of South African boxing champions. He made his professional debut in May 2006 and rose from that boxing tradition to become one of the country's most decorated modern fighters.

He held the IBF junior-bantamweight title from 2014 to 2015 and the WBO bantamweight title from 2017 to 2019. He finished his career 29-4 with 22 KOs and one no contest across 34 professional fights.

His signature moment came in Belfast in November 2017. Tete is best remembered for his 11-second knockout of fellow South African Siboniso Gonya in Belfast in November 2017, which remains the fastest stoppage in a world title fight in boxing history — a Guinness World Record he still held at the time of his death.

His death comes just weeks after the end of the four-year ban he received for testing positive for the anabolic steroid stanozolol following his 2022 fight with Jason Cunningham. His suspension ended on July 29 and he had been planning a return to the ring.

Rest in peace.

Arozarena's Day: First Pitch, Last Pitch, and a Robbery in Between

Randy Arozarena capped his day with a walk-off homer to stun the Cubs, 5-4. He also homered on the very first pitch he saw after being moved to leadoff when Brendan Donovan was a late scratch. And somewhere between those two home runs, he robbed Miguel Amaya in the third inning to back Kade Anderson in his MLB debut.

First pitch of the day: 416 feet to center. Later in the game: robs a home run. Ninth inning, down two, two on, two out: 404 feet to center for the walk-off.

Leadoff home run on first pitch and walk-off home run in same game: 8/22/26 Randy Arozarena, 8/17/26 Pete Crow-Armstrong, 5/7/57 Vic Power.

That's the entire list. Three names in the recorded history of Major League Baseball. Two of them did it in the last six days.

The Vic Power line sitting there from 1957 for almost seven decades, untouched, and then two guys knock it down inside a week. Baseball found a new bit and ran it back immediately.

Arozarena wasn't even supposed to lead off. Brendan Donovan was scratched after getting hit in the head by a baseball during batting practice. Randy moved up in the order and promptly sent the first pitch he saw 416 feet.

Bills Announce Death of Ed Oliver's 2-Year-Old Son

The Buffalo Bills announced on Saturday that defensive tackle Ed Oliver's 2-year-old son, also named Ed, has died.

Buffalo Bills defensive lineman Ed Oliver has been carrying a heavy burden over the past two months as it became public on Saturday that he has been mourning the loss of his 2-year-old son, who died in a drowning accident in June. The toddler was pronounced dead after being found unresponsive in a residential pool in Harris County, Texas, on June 28, a person briefed on what happened told The Associated Press.

"On behalf of the entire Buffalo Bills organization, we want to publicly express our condolences to Ed Oliver and his family on the devastating loss of his son," Bills general manager Brandon Beane said in a statement. "It's an unthinkable tragedy for any parent. We will continue to support Ed and his family as we all grieve this tragic loss."

Oliver has been grieving throughout training camp and many of his teammates only learned of the tragic accident on Friday. Following Saturday's 31-7 preseason win over the Browns, Bills quarterback Josh Allen said the team is standing with Oliver. "Obviously, very unfortunate what happened and as a teammate of him, as someone in this locker room, all we can do is love on him," Allen said.

Oliver has asked for privacy and prayers at this very difficult time.

Butker Boots a 69-Yarder That Doesn't Count

Harrison Butker hit the longest field goal in NFL history on Saturday night. Then everyone politely explained to him it doesn't count.

Butker drilled a 69-yard field goal just before halftime in the Chiefs' game against the Buccaneers. The NFL record is 68 yards. Butker topped it. But preseason kicks don't officially count, so Cam Little's 68-yarder from last season still stands as the longest kick in NFL history.

The kick may have been good from more than 70 yards. Sixty-nine yards is roughly the distance from the back of your own end zone to your opponent's 31. He hit it with room to spare in a game that will be forgotten by Tuesday.

Cam Little also made a 70-yard field goal during last year's preseason. Then he went out and broke the real record in November anyway. So the preseason 70-yarder is a lead indicator, not a consolation prize.

The rest of Butker's night was a reminder that leg strength and clutch kicking are two different skills. Butker missed a game-winning attempt in the 16-15 loss. Hit from 69, missed from 43. Preseason football, everybody.

Casey Mize Slipped Down the Dugout Steps

Casey Mize is a former No. 1 overall pick. He was the centerpiece return in a Padres deadline trade. He is in the middle of a playoff race in San Diego. He got hurt by stairs.

Casey Mize sprained his right ankle — his "drive" leg off the mound — while descending the dugout steps between the second and third innings of his start against the Twins.

In his own words: "I think my spike got caught on like the edge of the step, I guess, and then it kind of just made me slip a little bit. I tried to catch myself, and I ended up rolling my ankle kind of on the bottom step there."

Only in baseball does a franchise pitcher get taken out by architecture. Imaging on his injured ankle was negative, and Mize is classified as day-to-day for now. Small mercy for a Padres team that under first-year manager Craig Stammen is 70-60 and in third wild-card position in the NL. They're also 14-6 thus far in August.

He now has a 7.00 ERA after four starts with the Padres. His pre-trade work with the Tigers — a 2.70 ERA and 2.56 FIP over 16 starts — gives him a strong overall body of work this season. So he was already struggling to adjust to San Diego and now the dugout got him too. Rough month.

Kentucky's Portal Reload Now Looks Like a Final Four Roster

Mark Pope just added Mark Mitchell. Kentucky's summer went from "questioned" to "loaded" in about a week.

Mark Mitchell — cleared to play a fifth season of college basketball through a temporary restraining order — has committed to Kentucky. Less than 24 hours after the injunction, Mitchell was in the portal. Kentucky pounced.

Mitchell is the latest proven college player to double back to school after having a cup of coffee in Summer League. He played with the Denver Nuggets, but with no certainty of making an NBA roster this fall, he and his representation took to the courts to sue for a fifth year. Judge grants the injunction, portal cracks open for a week, Kentucky pounces.

Mitchell's résumé is real. Five-star and McDonald's All-American coming out of high school. Started 67 games over two seasons at Duke. Then to Missouri, where he went for 21 points on 7-15 shooting, four assists and one rebound in a 73-68 win at Rupp Arena, then dropped 32 on 13-21 with seven rebounds against Kentucky in the SEC Tournament. The year before — also an All-SEC season — he went for 22 points on 8-14 shooting with seven rebounds and three assists. He's been a thorn in UK's side for two years. Now he's joining forces.

Mitchell's arrival will turn Kentucky into a legitimate Final Four contender months after Mark Pope's offseason recruiting had been questioned.

One caveat sitting in the corner of the room: The NCAA has vowed to fight all federal and state cases filed by those athletes seeking a fifth season, meaning Mitchell's case could be appealed and his eligibility could be rescinded, which occurred in the Colorado ruling. So enjoy the roster while the injunction holds.
