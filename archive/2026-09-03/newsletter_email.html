The Clippers Kawhi Heist: Five Picks Gone, Uncle Dennis Banned, Kawhi Fined $700K

The NBA finally dropped the hammer on the Clippers today. Five first-round picks forfeited. Ballmer suspended for a year. Kawhi fined $700K and traded to Toronto.

@ShamsCharania
Per NBA: The Clippers shall forfeit five first-round draft picks, one in each of the 2029, 2030, 2031, 2032, and 2033 NBA Drafts.
Clippers owner Steve Ballmer is suspended from all league and team activities for one year for knowingly seeking to help Mr. Leonard obtain off-court income opportunities, for approving a business deal that he knew was a precondition for Aspiration to enter into an endorsement agreement with Mr. Leonard, and for his failure to create conditions under which his organization abided by the NBA's circumvention rules.
Clippers President of Business Operations Gillian Zucker is suspended without pay for one year for being primarily and directly culpable for the impermissible endorsement arrangements and for providing false and misleading statements to investigators.
Clippers President of Basketball Operations Lawrence Frank is suspended without pay for six months for his involvement with the impermissible endorsement arrangements and for approving impermissible expenses incurred by Mr. Leonard and his family.
The Clippers organization and personnel are subject to a compliance and monitoring program overseen by the league office for a period of five years.

Five first-round picks. A $30M fine to Ballmer. A one-year suspension for the richest owner in American sports. Zucker gone for a year, Frank gone for six months, Uncle Dennis banned from team business for five years. Kawhi himself: a $700K fine and a plane ticket to Toronto, where the trade is expected to close in the coming days.

September 2021: the Clippers announced a $300 million partnership with Aspiration, a green banking company Ballmer had personally invested in. Two months later, Aspiration was introduced to Kawhi Leonard — three months after the Clippers had agreed to a four-year, $173 million extension with him. Leonard then agreed to a four-year, $28 million endorsement deal in April 2022 through his LLC, KL2 Aspire.

The clause: the deal between Aspiration and KL2 Aspire would be voided if Leonard left the Clippers, and Leonard could "decline to proceed with any action desired" by Aspiration and continue to be paid. No appearances. No marketing. No social posts. A $28M endorsement contingent on staying a Clipper and doing nothing.

A $28M endorsement contingent on staying a Clipper and doing nothing in return.

Clippers minority owner Dennis J. Wong invested almost $2 million into Aspiration nine days before Aspiration wired $1.75 million to Leonard's KL2 Aspire LLC on Dec. 15, 2022. A former Aspiration employee told Pablo Torre that 20% of Aspiration's staff was laid off on the same day Leonard received his payment.

Aspiration laid off a fifth of its workforce and used the money to pay Kawhi. The company then filed for bankruptcy. Aspiration co-founder Joe Sanberg pleaded guilty to two counts of wire fraud, admitting he defrauded investors and lenders of more than $248 million.

Kate Koval Breaks Silence on LSU Exit

Kate Koval finally said why she left LSU. Kim Mulkey signed a Russian player after the transfer portal deadline had already closed. Koval, a native of Ukraine whose home country is still at war with Russia, found out too late to make a normal move.

Koval didn't lose minutes, didn't get benched, didn't want a bigger role. She was asked to share a locker room with a Russian teammate while her family and country live under bombardment, and the timing of the signing meant she couldn't even transfer clean. Every option after the portal closed was worse than the last.

Mulkey has recruited from all over the world and always will. But you don't do it three days after your Ukrainian center runs out of exits.
