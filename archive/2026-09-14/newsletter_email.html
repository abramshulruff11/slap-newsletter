Zverev Wins US Open, Extends American Men's Grand Slam Drought to 23 Years

Alexander Zverev started 2026 with zero Grand Slam titles. He's ending it with two.

Zverev beat Ben Shelton 6-3, 7-6(2), 5-7, 6-2 in three hours and 33 minutes. US Open title to go with the French Open he won in June. The last player to win multiple majors in a single season was Jannik Sinner in 2024. Before that, the Big Three basically had a subscription. Zverev spent a decade being the guy who was supposed to break through and never did — three lost Slam finals, a two-sets-to-love collapse against Thiem in the 2020 US Open final, enough "when will it happen for him?" columns to fill a library. Now he's a two-Slam guy in three months.

His postmatch line said it out loud:

- "We went 30 years without winning a Grand Slam title, and now we won two within a span of three months."

Meanwhile, on the other side of the net, the American men's Grand Slam drought hits 23 years.

Andy Roddick beat Juan Carlos Ferrero in straight sets at the 2003 US Open. More than two decades later, Roddick remains the most recent American man to win a major. Nadal was making his first US Open main draw appearance as an unknown teenager. Federer had one major. Djokovic was 16. Instagram didn't exist. The iPhone didn't exist. The Big Three ran off 66 majors in the meantime and are all effectively retired, and Americans STILL can't get one.

Shelton did give the crowd a moment. He took the third set 7-5 — an American man won a set in a Major final for the first time since Roddick at Wimbledon in 2009. That's how bad the drought is. Not "first title in decades." Not even "first final appearance in years." First SET won in a Major final in 17 years.

Then he lost the fourth 6-2 and the vibe was gone.

The one genuinely fun moment came at the trophy ceremony. Trinity Rodman was in the stands supporting her boyfriend. Zverev, mic in hand, turned to her.

- Zverev to Rodman: "Trinity, I thought you had a game today… You're the lucky charm… not today, but usually."

Beat her boyfriend in four sets and roasted her from the podium. Cold.

Cardinals Upset Chargers, End Debut Curse

Mike LaFleur is the first Arizona Cardinals head coach to win his debut in decades.

LaFleur, 39, was not even born the last time a Cardinals coach won his debut. Don Coryell won his in 1973 when the franchise was still in St. Louis. The 12 coaches after Coryell all lost their debut. Twelve straight coaches. Ken Whisenhunt lost his. Bruce Arians lost his. LaFleur just ended it by beating the Chargers in Inglewood.
