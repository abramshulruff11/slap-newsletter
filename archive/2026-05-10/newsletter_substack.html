Bobby Cox, Hall of Fame Braves Manager Who Won 1995 World Series, Dies at 84

Bobby Cox, who managed the Braves to 14 consecutive division titles and the 1995 World Series championship, died Saturday at 84.

Fourth all-time in managerial wins with 2,504 victories. He led the Braves to 14 consecutive division titles from 1991 to 2005, five National League pennants and the World Series title in 1995.

One of the defining figures of baseball in the 1990s. Commissioner Rob Manfred: "Bobby Cox led one of the greatest eras of sustained excellence in baseball history. As manager of the Braves, his clubs became an October fixture, representing consistency, professionalism, and championship-caliber baseball for an entire generation of fans."

https://twitter.com/espn/status/2053270703923675154

Cox was known for wearing spikes and stirrups in the dugout, for sporadic bursts of anger which prompted a record number of ejections, and for being at the center of one of the most dominant runs in baseball history.

He holds the MLB record for managerial ejections with 162, often defending his players passionately from the dugout. Unlike John McGraw before him, Cox did not have a reputation for having a fiery temper — Cox generally only got ejected to prevent his players from being ejected.

Cox managed Hall of Famers Greg Maddux, Tom Glavine, John Smoltz and Chipper Jones. He was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 2014 alongside Maddux and Glavine.

The 1990s Braves remain one of the great dynasties that won only one championship. Fourteen straight division titles. Five pennants. One ring. They should have won more. They didn't. That's baseball.

Cox's death came days after the passing of media tycoon Ted Turner, who owned the Braves for a large chunk of Cox's time with the team.

Sean Strickland Upsets Khamzat Chimaev to Win UFC Middleweight Title at UFC 328

Sean Strickland won the middleweight title via split decision (48-47, 48-47, 47-48) over Khamzat Chimaev at UFC 328. Chimaev lost for the first time in 16 professional fights.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2053340860507463837

Strickland was a massive underdog. This is his second huge upset in a title fight. He beat Israel Adesanya in 2023, then handed Chimaev his first career loss to reclaim the middleweight title.

https://twitter.com/HaterReport/status/2053349528342794735

https://twitter.com/OldTakesExposed/status/2053325746865918224

Chimaev dominated the opening frame with his wrestling. But Strickland stuffed two labored shots early in the second, controlling Chimaev from top position. The third and fourth were contested exclusively on the feet. In the fifth, Chimaev opened with a takedown, only for Strickland to quickly work his way free before the two traded on the feet. When the scorecards were collected and tallied, it was Strickland that landed on the happy side of the split decision verdict.

The fighters tapped gloves to start the fight, and the prefight hostilities never spilled over into the cage.

Steve Kerr Agrees to 2-Year Extension, Remains Highest-Paid Coach in NBA

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2053268603995095087

https://twitter.com/ShamsCharania/status/2053268848594354525

After three weeks of extensive conversations, Kerr and the Warriors agreed to the deal. This new deal keeps Kerr as the highest-paid coach in the NBA annually.

The Warriors finished the season with a 37-45 record as the 10th seed and were eliminated by the Phoenix Suns in the play-in round. Kerr has a career record of 604-353 and a 104-48 playoff mark, with Golden State winning its last championship in 2022.

Specifically, the two sides reached an agreement on key issues including improved team discipline and accountability, offensive philosophies, and a full commitment to maximizing the roster around Stephen Curry.

Steph Curry is 38. Jimmy Butler tore his ACL in January. Draymond Green is 36. This is a "one more run" deal.

It's a sign that the Warriors are still all in on Curry. You don't keep Steve Kerr if you're looking to rebuild, but he's arguably their best option to keep the status quo and make one final push for another championship.

Hurricanes Complete Back-to-Back Sweeps, First Team to Start Playoffs 8-0 This Century

Jackson Blake scored at 5:31 of overtime, and the Carolina Hurricanes defeated the Philadelphia Flyers 3-2 to complete the Eastern Conference Second Round sweep on Saturday.

https://twitter.com/SportsCenter/status/2053285840600645983

Carolina is now 8-0 in the playoffs — the fifth team in NHL history to have an 8-0 start to the postseason.

https://twitter.com/StatMuse/status/2053283377130696844

It marked the first time that a team has gone undefeated in the first two rounds of the playoffs since the NHL's four-round, seven-game series format was established in 1987. The last team to start the postseason 8-0 was the 1985 Edmonton Oilers, who won their first nine games. The only other teams to win their first eight were the 1969 St. Louis Blues, the 1960 Montreal Canadiens, and the 1952 Detroit Red Wings.

The Hurricanes, who reached the Eastern Conference finals for the third time in four years, are looking for their first Stanley Cup since 2006. Carolina is the 24th team in league history to win eight or more consecutive games during a playoff run. Eighteen of them have won the Stanley Cup.

Blake has made the conference final in each of his first two full NHL seasons. "The culture and the group we have this year, it's another level, I think, from even last year. I think last year, we had a great group and we were really tight-knit, but this year just feels a little different."
