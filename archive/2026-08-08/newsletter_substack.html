Red Sox Win 9th Straight Behind Payton Tolle's 14-K Gem, Now 27-3 in Last 30

Payton Tolle just carved his name into a list that only has one other Red Sox rookie on it. And that rookie's name was Roger Clemens.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2085902454151917955

14 strikeouts. Six innings. No walks. 13-1 final over the Athletics. Tolle's 14 strikeouts are tied for the second-most in a game by an American League rookie over the last 50 years, trailing only Roger Clemens, who fanned 15 in August 1984. Half a century of AL rookies. One name in front of him.

The line gets more absurd the longer you look at it. Only MLB pitcher since pitch tracking started in 1988 to record 14+ strikeouts in a game on fewer than 85 pitches. Most by a Red Sox pitcher, ever, in a six-inning start. 24 swings and misses — most by a Red Sox pitcher in more than three years.

Payton Tolle deciding whether to strike out the side or work on his cutter between pitches:

The Clemens shadow gets darker. Tolle became the second pitcher in Red Sox history, age 23 or younger, to record 12+ strikeouts without walking a batter. The other? Roger Clemens in the first of his two 20-strikeout games in 1986 against Seattle.

Now for the team behind him.

https://twitter.com/statmuse/status/2085906301012349024

27-3 in a 30-game stretch. Seventh team since 1920 to win 27 or more in a 30-game span. Only one to begin the run under .500.

Every other team that did this was already good and then got hotter. Boston was mediocre and then decided to stop losing baseball games entirely.

https://twitter.com/ClutchPoints/status/2085906979629797548

Even the guy catching Tolle is telling reporters this feels like catching a Cy Young winner. Jake Rogers, acquired from the Orioles earlier this week in the Adley Rutschman blockbuster, enjoyed being on Tolle's side this time in his first start for his new team. Rogers said it reminded him of a different power lefty he used to catch in Detroit named Tarik Skubal. "That's how it felt tonight."

Waldschmidt Walk-Off Sinks Dodgers for 7th Straight Loss

The Dodgers were three outs from finally ending it.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2085945190620000361

Ryan Waldschmidt, Diamondbacks rookie, second career home run. Two-run shot with one out in the bottom of the ninth. 4-3 final at Chase Field.

The details are worse for LA than the score. Los Angeles clawed back and briefly took the lead in the eighth on an Andy Pages go-ahead homer. Then Nolan Arenado struck out with the bases loaded in the same inning. They led. They lost. Again.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2085945834416316907

The Diaz situation is its own crisis. Since returning from injury, four games, five runs in 3.0 innings. Six hits. Two walks. Two home runs. A closer with an infinity ERA is a fun toy for a first-place team. For a team on a seven-game skid, it's a resignation letter.

Elsewhere in the same building, Kevin Gausman made his Cubs debut against the Royals and was excellent — 7.0 IP, 4 H, 2 ER, 1 BB, 4 SO — but that story got completely eaten by whatever is happening to Los Angeles right now.

Yankees Erase 9th-Inning Deficit, Walk Off Braves in the 10th

Max Fried pitched 7 shutout innings against his former team. And it didn't even matter.

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2085925141964259516

Fried left with a scoreless tie. The bullpen gave up the Riley homer. Then this happened:

https://twitter.com/TalkinBaseball_/status/2085937299712344084

Ben Rice ripped a tying single off the right-field fence with two outs in the ninth. Ryan McMahon hit a game-winning sacrifice fly in the 10th. 3-2 final. The Yankees rallied past the Atlanta Braves in a rain-delayed classic.
