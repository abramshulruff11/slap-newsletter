Chris Sale, 37, Threw 100 MPH for the First Time Since 2018 and Shut Out the Dodgers

Chris Sale hadn't finished a game in seven years. He hadn't thrown 100 mph in eight. Thursday night at Truist Park he did both. Against the two-time defending champs.

9 IP. 0 R. 11 K. 0 BB. 109 pitches. He struck out Shohei Ohtani twice. In the third inning, Sale reached back and fired a 100.1 mph fastball — the first time he'd touched triple digits since 2018.

Sixteen years in the league and the man still answers postgame questions like a substitute teacher who wants to end the interview.

At 37 years and 150 days, Sale became just the sixth pitcher since 1900 at that age or older to record 10-plus strikeouts and issue no walks in a shutout. The most recent: Randy Johnson. 2004 and twice in 2001. Sale's childhood idol.

The last time Sale threw a complete game he was in a Red Sox uniform. Since then: Tommy John, a broken rib, a Cy Young at 35. He barely pitched from 2020 through 2023. He shouldn't be doing any of this.

MLB Wants to Squeeze Free Agency Into Two Weeks. The MLBPA Said "Entirely Unserious."

@JeffPassan
Major League Baseball today proposed drastic changes to its offseason calendar, including shortening free agency to a two- to three-week window every offseason, sources tell ESPN. The details of the proposal:

- A free agent dead period until the first day of the Winter Meetings in early December in which players and teams can negotiate but no deals can be signed. Trades would be allowed during this period
- Free agency would open the Monday the Winter Meetings begin and would close Dec. 21.
- From Dec. 21 through the first Monday in February, no free agent signings or trades would be allowed
- Free agents could sign and trades would be allowed again from the first Monday in February onward
View tweet

Dead period until December. Free agency opens for roughly two weeks. Freeze from Dec. 21 until February. If you're wondering where the salary cap fits into this, the MLBPA already figured it out.

@JeffPassan
MLBPA interim executive director Bruce Meyer on MLB's proposal that would overhaul the offseason calendar and shrink most of free agency to a short window in December:

"The league, through their propaganda arm, is promoting their latest set of roster and transaction proposals as player-friendly.

"MLB expressly conditioned these proposals on the players' agreement to a salary cap system, which would drastically reduce overall player compensation, eliminate fully guaranteed contracts through escrow, and turn back the clock on fundamental player rights.

"Their latest proposals regarding free agency would eliminate Player rights by establishing an artificial free agency window, subject to a salary cap, which would reduce player leverage and create a game of musical chairs pitting player vs. player.

"The suggestion that the only way to limit roster churn, or incentivize earlier Free Agent signings is with a salary cap is entirely unserious. Players have proposed changes that would address these priorities, but the league is not interested in any changes that don't directly reduce the rights of all players and shift leverage to the owners."
View tweet

"Propaganda arm." "Entirely unserious." That is not the language of a labor negotiation about to go smoothly. Circle 2026-2027 offseason on the calendar.
