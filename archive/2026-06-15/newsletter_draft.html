Carolina Hurricanes Win the Stanley Cup, 20 Years Later

The Carolina Hurricanes are Stanley Cup champions. Game 6 in Vegas: 3-0. Brandon Bussi shutout, 22 saves. Series over.

Twenty years between Cups for this franchise. The same guy lifted both of them. Brind'Amour became the first Hurricanes player to touch the Stanley Cup in 2006 as captain. He just won it again as head coach. He is the seventh person in NHL history to lift the Stanley Cup as a player and head coach for the same team, and the first since 1956.

1956. Eisenhower administration. The last guy to do this was Toe Blake. Brind'Amour just joined a list that hadn't added a name in 70 years.

Slavin became the second American and eighth player to hoist the Cup and take home an Olympic gold medal in the same year. Ken Morrow was the only U.S. player to do it. He was on the 1980 "Miracle on Ice" team and a member of the New York Islanders as it started its dynasty.

Forty-six years. That's how long Morrow sat as the only American to pull this off. "I used to joke I was a trivia question for a lot of years: Who's the only guy that ever did that? Only player I was at that time, not just American, but the only player," Morrow told The Associated Press last week.

The Knicks ended a 53-year title drought on Saturday. The Hurricanes ended a 20-year drought on Sunday.

Brandon Bussi made his first career playoff start in Game 4 of the Cup Final. Three games later he had a road shutout to clinch a championship.

Raleighwood is up. The Cup is going home.
